1 · Critical Minerals · Texas Manufacturing

U.S.–China talks raise the strategic value of Texas rare-earth projects

What happened: Sunday’s U.S.–China talks include critical-mineral flows and rare-earth supply. Texas already has two major state-backed projects positioned directly against foreign dependence: USA Rare Earth’s Round Top project in Hudspeth County, representing more than $1.4 billion and 260 expected jobs, and MP Materials’ Northlake magnet campus, representing more than $1.25 billion and 1,500+ jobs.

Why it matters: Rare-earth magnets sit inside motors, robotics, semiconductor equipment, defense systems, data centers and advanced manufacturing. A supply disruption or new trade restriction can quickly change sourcing economics.

Who may benefit / hurt: West Texas mining, North Texas magnet manufacturing, construction and specialty processing can gain. Manufacturers dependent on China-origin rare-earth inputs remain exposed.

Operator move: Map magnet, motor and rare-earth demand around Texas semiconductor, data-center and industrial projects and qualify for mine, processing, electrical and plant-build scopes.
Risk check: Today’s U.S.–China talks are not an outcome; Texas project job and investment figures remain subject to project execution and grant conditions.

2 · Fort Worth · Carrier

Carrier’s proposed $433.8M manufacturing plant enters its final site-selection stretch

What happened: Fort Worth approved a seven-year tax-abatement package for Carrier Global’s proposed Alliance-area plant. City-record reporting describes $433.8 million of investment and 495 jobs with at least a $75,000 average salary; Carrier’s final site decision is expected by September 30.

Why it matters: The plant would deepen North Texas manufacturing around HVAC, thermal management and AI/data-center cooling.

Who may benefit / hurt: Sheet metal, electrical, controls, automation, testing, construction and logistics suppliers can gain. Competing locations and local employers competing for skilled labor may lose.

Operator move: Finish supplier and construction qualification packages before month-end; emphasize cooling, power, controls, fabrication and commissioning capability.
Risk check: Fort Worth approved incentives, but Carrier has not made its final site choice. Performance conditions apply.

3 · Fort Worth · HARTING

September 29 vote could advance a $192.7M connector manufacturing campus

What happened: Fort Worth City Council is scheduled to consider a tax-abatement agreement for HARTING on September 29. The proposed project calls for about $192.7 million of capital investment and more than 700 jobs, with manufacturing, cable assembly and U.S. headquarters functions.

Why it matters: Industrial connectors are used across automation, rail, power, renewable energy, machinery and data-center infrastructure, making the project a broad supplier opportunity.

Who may benefit / hurt: Cable, plastics, stamping, metalworking, automation, electrical and construction firms could gain; competing states and Fort Worth employers may face labor competition.

Operator move: Prepare connector-component, cable-assembly, automation and plant-build pitches before the September 29 council vote.
Risk check: HARTING has not made a final Fort Worth location decision; other states are competing for the project.

4 · Fort Bend · Tesla Solar Manufacturing

Project Crystal Sun advances locally, but Texas still has to win the site

What happened: Lamar CISD approved the JETI agreement for Tesla’s proposed $10.1 billion vertically integrated solar manufacturing project. The application projects 9,712 full-time jobs by 2033, about $1.3 billion in annual payroll and peak construction employment above 1,100.

Why it matters: If selected, Fort Bend would gain a huge manufacturing stack spanning ingot, wafer, cell and module production rather than module assembly alone.

Who may benefit / hurt: Electrical, process-water, clean manufacturing, automation, materials handling, industrial construction and logistics firms could benefit; roads, utilities and nearby communities would carry execution pressure.

Operator move: Qualify now for high-voltage electrical, clean manufacturing, process water, automation, material handling and construction packages.
Risk check: Tesla is still evaluating an out-of-state alternative and final state/site approvals remain outstanding.
Houston Chronicle · Sep. 18
5 · South Texas · Encinal Power

A reported 6.3-GW Encinal power project would be a Texas construction megaproject if it advances

What happened: Reuters reports a combined-cycle gas-power project in Encinal is among projects under discussion in South Korea’s U.S. investment package. Media reports describe roughly 6.3 GW and about $22.3 billion of investment.

Why it matters: A project of that scale would drive gas, turbines, substations, transmission, switchgear, cooling, water, controls, EPC and heavy construction.

Who may benefit / hurt: Texas generation, gas, electrical and civil contractors could gain; competing generation projects and constrained water/transmission resources may face pressure.

Operator move: Prepare generation, pipeline, electrical, water and EPC capability packages so they are ready if procurement begins.
Risk check: South Korean officials say details are not set. This is not a final investment decision or a notice to proceed.
Reuters · Sep. 18
6 · West Texas · Natural Gas

West Texas Express has five days left in its shipper open season

What happened: Enbridge’s non-binding open season closes September 25 for a proposed 150-plus-mile pipeline from Waha toward El Paso, Hudspeth County and U.S.–Mexico border delivery points, with initial capacity up to 2 Bcf/d and a targeted Q4 2029 in-service date.

Why it matters: More Permian takeaway can support production, West Texas power demand, industrial loads and Mexico gas exports while creating years of pipeline construction and service work.

Who may benefit / hurt: Compression, valves, metering, electrical, ROW, trenching and civil contractors may gain; competing takeaway systems face commercial pressure.

Operator move: Map likely contractor scopes, border delivery nodes and equipment packages before the September 25 close.
Risk check: The open season is non-binding; commercial support, routing, permits and final investment approval are still required.
Enbridge · official open seasonReuters · Sep. 15
7 · Austin + Killeen · Foreign-Trade Zones

Two semiconductor FTZ clocks create immediate customs and logistics work

What happened: Ichor Systems’ Austin production matter has an October 5 comment deadline, while Dongjin Semichem Texas’ Killeen matter remains open through October 20.

Why it matters: Semiconductor equipment and specialty chemicals carry high-value imported inputs where duty deferral, re-export treatment and inventory timing can materially change working capital.

Who may benefit / hurt: Import-intensive manufacturers, customs specialists and bonded logistics can gain where savings exceed compliance cost; weak HTS/origin controls can create penalties or eliminate savings.

Operator move: Offer SKU-level HTS/origin modeling, FTZ feasibility, bonded inventory controls and specialty-chemical logistics.
Risk check: Section 232/301 and privileged-foreign-status treatment can change the outcome.

8 · Incentives · OZ + JETI + Enterprise Zone

Texas operators can now layer three different incentive screens into site selection

What happened: Texas nominated 605 tracts across 105 counties for Opportunity Zone 2.0. JETI provides enhanced school-tax treatment for qualifying projects in qualified Opportunity Zones, while the next Texas Enterprise Zone application deadline is December 1.

Why it matters: Capital-gains treatment, school-tax limitations and sales/use-tax refunds can materially alter project economics when the underlying site already works on utilities and labor.

Who may benefit / hurt: Capital-intensive projects can benefit from properly stacked programs; speculative buyers can overpay for incentive labels without infrastructure fundamentals.

Operator move: Build one site matrix combining power, water, labor, OZ, JETI, FTZ, Enterprise Zone, rail/highway, flood risk and local abatements.
Risk check: Treasury certification, JETI approval and Enterprise Zone designation are separate processes with different eligibility rules.

9 · Gulf Coast · Mexico Fuel Trade

Pemex refinery weakness keeps Texas fuel-export infrastructure strategically relevant

What happened: Reuters reported Pemex’s Q2 diesel output fell about 11% and gasoline output about 13%, while Mexico increased costly refined-product imports.

Why it matters: Mexico’s refinery performance directly affects demand for Gulf Coast refining, storage, terminals, marine loading and South Texas fuel distribution.

Who may benefit / hurt: Texas refiners, terminal operators, storage and logistics providers can benefit if import demand remains elevated; Mexican fuel-intensive businesses face higher cost exposure.

Operator move: Monitor export-terminal utilization, storage demand, marine schedules and border-distribution contracts tied to Mexican fuel buyers.
Risk check: Refinery utilization can recover; elevated imports should not be assumed permanent.
Reuters · Sep. 17
10 · Socorro · Borderplex AI Manufacturing

Amazon + Wiwynn give Texas a live USMCA AI-hardware localization platform

What happened: Amazon and Wiwynn are expanding server-system and rack manufacturing in Socorro, targeting nearly 1,000 additional advanced-manufacturing jobs by the end of 2027. Wiwynn says investment in the Texas facility exceeds $1.6 billion.

Why it matters: Washington’s push for tighter North American content and traceability in AI hardware could increase the value of the El Paso–Socorro–Juárez manufacturing corridor.

Who may benefit / hurt: Regional rack, sheet-metal, busway, power, cooling, cable, test and logistics suppliers may gain; non-regional subassembly suppliers face localization pressure.

Operator move: Build a Borderplex BOM map and target high-value components that can be manufactured, finished or tested within the Texas–Juárez corridor.
Risk check: Job targets and final USMCA AI-origin rules remain forward-looking.
