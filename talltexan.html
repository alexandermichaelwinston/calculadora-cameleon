1 · Statewide · Data Centers

Texas' data-center permit freeze raises the diligence bar for powered land

What happened: Governor Greg Abbott directed TCEQ to halt new state-issued permits sought by data centers until ERCOT completes its audit. Reuters reported more than 470 GW of data centers and other large loads in the interconnection queue.

Why it matters: Queue position alone does not make a site truly power-ready. Water, permitting, upgrade responsibility and audit evidence now matter more before land or construction commitments.

Who may benefit / hurt: Water-reuse, efficiency, on-site generation, power-quality and compliance providers may gain. Speculative powered-land developers can face delay and repricing.

Operator move: Require written ERCOT/interconnection status, audit status, TCEQ permit path, water supply, transformer/substation scope and cost-allocation evidence before closing.
Risk check: The effect varies by project and permit. Future incentive reductions discussed by state leaders would require separate legislative action.
Reuters · Sep. 21
2 · Encinal · Power + AI

South Korea-selected Encinal project could become a $20B+ Texas power build

What happened: South Korea selected a Texas gas-fired project as the first project under its $350 billion U.S. investment pact. Reuters reports the Encinal project at roughly 6.3 GW and more than $20 billion, intended to serve AI data centers and semiconductor factories.

Why it matters: A project this size can generate years of turbine, pipeline, substation, transmission, switchgear, cooling-water and heavy-civil work.

Who may benefit / hurt: Texas EPC, gas, electrical, fabrication and construction firms could gain; competing projects may face tighter skilled labor and equipment availability.

Operator move: Prepare pre-FID capability packages for gas supply, high voltage, controls, water, cooling, civil, commissioning and maintenance; identify Korean OEM/EPC relationships.
Risk check: Selection is not a final investment decision. U.S. investment-committee approval and further negotiations remain.
Reuters · Sep. 21
3 · Houston · Pharma Manufacturing

Lilly's $6.5B Houston API project creates a 4,000-job construction wave

What happened: Lilly broke ground September 21 at Generation Park on a $6.5 billion active-pharmaceutical-ingredient facility. Lilly expects more than 600 permanent high-wage jobs and about 4,000 construction jobs. Texas extended a $5.5 million TEF grant and approved the project under JETI.

Why it matters: This is a major domestic reshoring project in high-value pharmaceutical manufacturing and a large market for clean utilities, process systems, controls and specialty construction.

Who may benefit / hurt: Process-piping, electrical, automation, cleanroom, water, QA/validation and workforce-training firms may gain; other industrial employers may face skilled-labor competition.

Operator move: Pursue vendor registration for process piping, instrumentation, electrical, clean utilities, water, commissioning, validation, automation and long-term maintenance.
Risk check: Project capex and construction-job totals do not equal available subcontract awards; qualification and package timing must be verified.

4 · Border Agriculture · Santa Teresa

Santa Teresa cattle route reopens today — important for Texas Panhandle feedlots

What happened: The Santa Teresa, New Mexico livestock port reopened to Mexican cattle under enhanced screwworm safeguards. Initial volume is expected around 750 to 1,600 cattle per day, below historic peak throughput.

Why it matters: The crossing historically handles a large share of Mexican cattle and much of that feeder supply moves toward Texas feedlots. Reopening improves livestock availability and hauling activity after the long closure.

Who may benefit / hurt: Texas feedlots, haulers, veterinarians and processors may benefit; disease-control requirements add cost and future shutdown risk.

Operator move: Line up compliant trailers, traceability, inspection documents, treatment records and receiving capacity before increasing volume.
Risk check: New World screwworm has not been eradicated. USDA can adjust flows or pause the reopening if surveillance worsens.
Reuters-syndicated · Sep. 24
5 · Texas ↔ Mexico · USMCA Sales

Mexico's plan to buy more U.S. goods is a direct export lead for Texas manufacturers

What happened: President Claudia Sheinbaum said Mexico intends to increase U.S. imports and reduce purchases from other countries during the USMCA review.

Why it matters: Texas has a logistics advantage into northern Mexico through Laredo, El Paso, Eagle Pass, Pharr and other crossings. Machinery, chemicals, fabricated goods, food/ag inputs and industrial components could gain share.

Who may benefit / hurt: Texas exporters, distributors, contract manufacturers and border warehouses may benefit; suppliers dependent on non-regional content may face more competition or origin scrutiny.

Operator move: Create delivered-to-border and delivered-to-plant substitution quotes for Mexico buyers and document North American origin clearly.
Risk check: Mexico has not issued a mandatory buy-U.S. rule. Validate customer demand and final USMCA changes before adding capacity.
Reuters · Sep. 21
6 · West Texas · Natural Gas

West Texas Express open season closes tomorrow at 4 p.m. CST

What happened: Enbridge's non-binding open season for West Texas Express runs through September 25. The proposed greenfield pipeline would move Permian gas from Waha to the greater El Paso area, Hudspeth County and U.S.-Mexico border delivery points; prior reporting puts initial capacity at up to 2 Bcf/d.

Why it matters: Additional takeaway can support Permian production, El Paso power/industry and Mexican gas demand while creating pipeline and compression work.

Who may benefit / hurt: Pipeline, compression, valve, metering, electrical, trenching and ROW firms may gain if the project advances; competing takeaway systems face pressure.

Operator move: Map likely compressor, meter, electrical, civil, ROW and border-delivery packages now; shippers needing capacity should review the open-season service request.
Risk check: The open season is non-binding. Commercial support, route, permits and final investment decision remain outstanding.
Enbridge · official notice
7 · Austin + Killeen · Semiconductor FTZs

Two Texas semiconductor FTZ comment windows remain open

What happened: The FTZ Board lists Ichor Systems' Austin production application with comments due October 5 and Dongjin Semichem Texas' Killeen production application with comments due October 20.

Why it matters: Semiconductor equipment and specialty chemicals can carry significant imported-input and working-capital costs, making customs structure part of site economics.

Who may benefit / hurt: Customs specialists, bonded logistics, import-intensive manufacturers and domestic suppliers may gain; companies with weak HTS/origin controls face duty and compliance risk.

Operator move: Model HTS, origin, duty, dwell time, re-export share and special-tariff exposure SKU by SKU before assuming FTZ savings.
Risk check: Federal Register notices control; Section 232/301 and foreign-status treatment can change the outcome.
U.S. FTZ Board · current docket
8 · Statewide · OZ 2.0 + JETI

605 nominated Texas OZ tracts can potentially amplify JETI economics

What happened: Texas nominated 605 census tracts across 105 counties for the 2027-2036 Opportunity Zone cycle. Qualifying JETI projects in qualified Opportunity Zones are eligible for an additional 25% limitation on taxable value.

Why it matters: Federal capital-gains incentives plus school-tax limitations can materially affect industrial after-tax returns — if power, water, labor and logistics already work.

Who may benefit / hurt: Qualified manufacturing, high-tech infrastructure and dispatchable generation may benefit; speculative buyers can overpay for incentive-labeled land without operational fundamentals.

Operator move: Build one site score combining OZ, JETI, FTZ, power, water, flood risk, workforce, rail/highway and local abatements.
Risk check: Treasury certification and JETI approval are independent, and JETI has project-type, job and investment thresholds.

9 · Statewide · Grants + Enterprise Zone

Q4 incentive clock: DEAAG closes Nov. 6; Enterprise Zone closes Dec. 1

What happened: Texas' FY27 Defense Economic Adjustment Assistance Grant round accepts applications through November 6, with grants from $50,000 to $5 million. The next Texas Enterprise Zone application deadline is December 1.

Why it matters: Defense-community infrastructure funding and Enterprise Zone sales/use-tax refund designations can affect project financing and local economic-development packages.

Who may benefit / hurt: Eligible municipalities, defense communities and qualifying job/capex projects may benefit; late or incomplete applicants lose the round.

Operator move: Assemble community sponsorship, resolutions, job/capex commitments, matching-fund evidence and project schedules now rather than in the final week.
Risk check: Both programs are eligibility- and performance-based; an application is not an award.
Texas DEAAGTexas Enterprise Zone
10 · Laredo Corridor · Rail + Construction

Mexico's 396-km Saltillo-Nuevo Laredo rail build is now a live border-corridor opportunity

What happened: Mexico reports 9.86% physical progress on the 396-km Saltillo-Nuevo Laredo railway, with estimated investment of MXN 175.7 billion. A separate 5G Standalone rail-communications tender spanning this corridor and Mexico-Querétaro-Irapuato has an award scheduled for September 25.

Why it matters: Better rail infrastructure connecting Monterrey and Nuevo Laredo can reinforce the broader Laredo trade corridor and create construction, telecom, station and border-development demand on the Mexican side with spillover opportunities in Texas logistics.

Who may benefit / hurt: Rail/civil contractors, telecom integrators, materials suppliers, border warehouses and freight-service companies may benefit; individual packages remain procurement-sensitive.

Operator move: Track the Mexico tenders and separately position Texas assets for warehousing, drayage, customs, maintenance and industrial services tied to higher corridor activity.
Risk check: Passenger-rail construction does not automatically equal freight capacity; verify each operating scope and tender rather than assuming freight benefits.
Proyectos México · Sep. 23
