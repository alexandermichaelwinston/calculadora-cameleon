1 · Power + AI Infrastructure

Texas is tightening the gate on data-center growth

What happened: Texas has halted some new data-center grid connections while utilities audit speculative “ghost demand,” and on September 3 Reuters reported a broader political push in Texas to reconsider incentives and impose tougher restrictions around data-center development.

Why it matters: Firm power, water, funded interconnection requests and credible load forecasts are becoming more valuable than a flashy site plan.

Who may benefit / hurt: Projects with committed capital, power solutions and strong community benefits may gain scarcity value; speculative developers may lose queue position and incentive support.

Operator move: market verified power, substations, generation, switchgear, water and interconnection readiness as core industrial real-estate assets.
Risk check: the policy debate is evolving and not every proposed restriction has become law.

2 · San Antonio · Advanced Manufacturing

IEM turns switchgear into a 3,000-job supplier hunt

What happened: Industrial Electric Mfg. is establishing a $200 million advanced-manufacturing facility at Brooks in San Antonio with up to 3,000 jobs; Texas extended a $3.36 million TEF grant tied to 448 new jobs.

Why it matters: Power-distribution equipment is a bottleneck across factories, grid upgrades and data centers, so local supplier capacity can matter more as Texas filters speculative projects from real ones.

Who may benefit / hurt: Enclosure fabricators, copper/busbar suppliers, controls, testing, MRO, logistics and skilled staffing firms may gain; suppliers without quality systems or working capital may struggle to qualify.

Operator move: build a Brooks-area supplier prospect list and prequalify for switchgear, sheet metal, wiring, controls and field-service packages.
Risk check: “up to” employment totals can phase over years and supplier awards are not guaranteed.
Texas Governor · Aug. 25
3 · Grimes County · Semiconductors

Terafab creates a Southeast Texas mega-project ecosystem

What happened: SpaceX announced a vertically integrated semiconductor fabrication plant in Grimes County. The first phase exceeds $16.8 billion in capital investment and is expected to create 3,000 jobs; Texas awarded a $30 million TEF grant and the project qualified under JETI.

Why it matters: A fab of this scale creates demand beyond chips — electrical infrastructure, water treatment, specialty gases, cleanrooms, automation, logistics, housing and supplier real estate.

Who may benefit / hurt: Qualified industrial contractors and fab suppliers may gain; developers who buy land without verified utilities can get trapped.

Operator move: identify tier-2 and tier-3 vendors that need satellite sites within practical drive time of Grimes County and screen only sites with real power and water capacity.
Risk check: later phases and total buildout timing are not guaranteed.
Texas Governor · Aug. 6
4 · Houston · Life Sciences

Bristol Myers Squibb makes Houston a pharmaceutical-construction target

What happened: Bristol Myers Squibb plans an approximately $2.3 billion Houston manufacturing campus with nearly 500 jobs. Texas extended a $4.89 million TEF grant and the expansion also qualifies under JETI.

Why it matters: Pharmaceutical manufacturing pulls demand for GMP construction, process piping, HVAC, validation, controls, water systems, specialized warehousing and regulated logistics.

Who may benefit / hurt: Specialty contractors and quality-certified suppliers can gain; ordinary commercial contractors may find pharma qualification barriers high.

Operator move: build a GMP-ready subcontractor team now and document QA/QC, safety, clean utility and validation experience before bid packages peak.
Risk check: vendor qualification is project-specific and far stricter than normal commercial work.
Texas Governor · Aug. 10
5 · Austin · Robotics

Amazon’s robotics factory deepens Central Texas automation demand

What happened: Amazon selected Austin for a multi-billion-dollar robotics manufacturing facility expected to create 300–500 manufacturing and engineering jobs.

Why it matters: Robotics pulls machine vision, precision machining, sensors, drives, controls, electronics, industrial software and test equipment into the local supplier map.

Who may benefit / hurt: Automation integrators and advanced suppliers can gain; low-complexity labor-only shops face more pressure as automation penetration rises.

Operator move: identify the one automation component or service your company can qualify for rather than trying to sell a generic “manufacturing” capability.
Risk check: supplier allocations, building scope and commissioning schedules can change.
Texas Governor · Aug. 19
6 · Customs + Logistics · Live Deadlines

Three Texas FTZ dockets are active right now

What happened: The U.S. FTZ Board lists Hithium Tech USA in Forney with comments due September 8, CGT in New Braunfels due September 14 and Abbott Laboratories in Irving due September 15.

Why it matters: FTZ activity can reveal where import-intensive manufacturing is clustering and where duty deferral, re-export treatment and inventory-control services may create value.

Who may benefit / hurt: Customs brokers, bonded logistics, warehouse systems and import-heavy manufacturers may gain; firms with minimal imported content may not justify the compliance overhead.

Operator move: run an FTZ savings model before choosing a lease near DFW, I-35 or other Texas logistics corridors.
Risk check: product classification, Section 232/301 rules and admission status determine actual savings.
U.S. FTZ Board · current docket
7 · Defense Communities · Grants

Texas opens FY27 defense infrastructure grants

What happened: The Defense Economic Adjustment Assistance Grant round opened September 1. Eligible Texas defense communities can seek $50,000 to $5 million per project, with applications due November 6.

Why it matters: The program can fund infrastructure tied to military value, resiliency, new missions and dual military/community benefit — often a catalyst for engineering, civil, utility and construction work.

Who may benefit / hurt: Municipalities, counties, defense authorities, junior colleges and planning commissions can benefit; private businesses must generally participate through eligible public applicants rather than apply directly.

Operator move: approach eligible communities now with shovel-ready infrastructure concepts and matching-fund strategies instead of waiting until the deadline month.
Risk check: submitted applications are capped at 50 pages and late applications are ineligible.
Texas Military Preparedness Commission
8 · Tax Refunds · Enterprise Zone

Missed the September Enterprise Zone round? December 1 is next

What happened: Texas’ Enterprise Zone Program provides state sales-and-use-tax refunds for qualifying investment and job-creation projects. After the September round, the next application deadline is December 1, 2026.

Why it matters: A local-government nomination can materially change effective project cost for expansions that already create qualifying full-time jobs.

Who may benefit / hurt: Capital-intensive employers meeting job and hiring criteria may gain; projects that cannot satisfy worker-eligibility and job-maintenance rules may not.

Operator move: start the municipal or county nomination packet now; collect hiring, wage, investment and qualified-site data before November.
Risk check: companies do not self-designate; communities nominate and all statutory criteria must be met.
Texas Enterprise Zone Program
9 · Semiconductors · Incentive Pipeline

Texas still has a nearly $948 million semiconductor incentive platform

What happened: Texas says the Semiconductor Innovation Fund has received approximately $948 million in total appropriations. Recent awards support projects including AOI in Sugar Land, Avant in Pharr, FormFactor in Farmers Branch, Coherent in Sherman and USA Rare Earth in Hudspeth County.

Why it matters: The opportunity is not one mega-fab; it is a statewide supplier chain spanning optics, memory, test, materials and critical minerals.

Who may benefit / hurt: Texas-based semiconductor and materials companies with real capex and job plans may gain; businesses without an established Texas presence are outside the core private-sector eligibility profile.

Operator move: map where your product fits the semiconductor stack — materials, fab equipment, test, optics, packaging, power or logistics — and pursue the regional cluster instead of one anchor tenant.
Risk check: grants are competitive and local support is one factor in evaluation.
Texas Semiconductor Innovation Fund
10 · Site Selection · JETI + OZ 2.0

Opportunity Zone 2.0 can amplify JETI — but only with the right site

What happened: Texas’ JETI program offers a 10-year school-district M&O appraised-value limitation for qualifying projects, and Comptroller guidance says a qualifying project entirely within an Opportunity Zone can receive the greater 75% taxable-value discount. OZ 2.0 begins January 1, 2027.

Why it matters: A tract that also has power, water, labor and logistics can support meaningful incentive stacking for large manufacturing, energy and technology projects.

Who may benefit / hurt: Large qualifying projects with well-chosen sites may gain; buyers who purchase land only because it is “OZ eligible” can be hurt if final designation or operating fundamentals fail.

Operator move: create one site-selection matrix with utilities, workforce, rail/highway, FTZ, OZ 2.0, JETI, local abatements and construction schedule before negotiating land.
Risk check: formal qualification and separate approvals are required; the entire project boundaries must satisfy JETI’s QOZ condition for the enhanced discount.
