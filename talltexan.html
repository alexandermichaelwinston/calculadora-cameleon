Nov 5 · North Texas electricity

Oncor deploys $6.3B through September, targets $9B this year

What happened: Oncor reported $572M Q3 net income on November 5 and approximately $6.3B spent on transmission/distribution capital through September; it expects roughly $9B in 2026 spending and projects its 2027–2031 base plan about 20% higher, pending finalization.

Why it matters: Power infrastructure governs data-center, industrial, residential and Permian electrification schedules across the service territory.

Who may benefit or be hurt: Qualified utility contractors, suppliers and skilled crews may gain; customers, landowners and projects awaiting approvals face cost and schedule risks.

Business move: Track PUCT certificates, transmission routes, equipment RFQs and Oncor supplier onboarding; target substation, line, vegetation and inspection work.
Risks / verification: The 293-GW data-center queue is requested load, not operating capacity; projected spending and routes remain conditional.

Nov 5 · Manufacturing cost signal

Texas manufacturers face a different labor-cost picture from the national average

What happened: BLS reports third-quarter U.S. manufacturing productivity rose only 0.3% annualized while manufacturing unit labor costs rose 2.7%, even as overall nonfarm productivity climbed 3.6%.

Why it matters: Texas factories competing for suppliers and skilled trades cannot assume economy-wide efficiency gains offset manufacturing wage bills.

Who may benefit or be hurt: Industrial automation, quality control and plant-efficiency vendors may benefit; poorly priced low-margin subcontractors may lose profitability.

Business move: Reprice industrial bids using labor hours per unit and verified wage escalation; offer pilots targeting waste and downtime.
Risks / verification: These are national manufacturing measures, not Texas-specific results; local occupations and subsectors may differ sharply.

Nov 5 · Building materials / tariffs

Canadian cabinet tariff risk threatens Texas renovation and housing bids

What happened: Reuters reports U.S. tariffs on Canadian kitchen cabinets are slated to rise from 25% to 50% January 1, 2027; several Canadian producers have already cut U.S. shipments and employment.

Why it matters: Custom-home, kitchen-remodeling and multi-family contractors in Texas may face changed supplier prices or longer procurement cycles.

Who may benefit or be hurt: Domestic cabinetmakers and alternative compliant suppliers may gain; builders with fixed-price Canadian cabinet allowances could lose margin.

Business move: Update bid allowance schedules and obtain dated supplier quotes plus HTS classification and tariff-allocation terms.
Risks / verification: Upcoming rates may change; not all cabinet SKUs or origin scenarios are identical. Validate exclusions before revising customer contracts.
Reuters · Nov 5 ↗
Nov 4 data · Texas border freight

$28.7B September U.S.–Mexico goods deficit underscores border exposure

What happened: The November 4 BEA/Census report shows a September $28.7B goods deficit with Mexico, separate from the national $105.0B goods-and-services deficit.

Why it matters: Texas border corridors handle substantial two-way commerce and need trucking reliability, customs expertise, secure yards and warehouse capacity.

Who may benefit or be hurt: Laredo/Pharr service firms, carriers and customs specialists may benefit; shippers face delays and duty/compliance risk.

Business move: Package cross-dock, track-and-trace and freight-visibility services for specific export/import lanes; benchmark actual arrival times.
Risks / verification: Trade deficit is not a measure of Texas freight volume or evidence of growth at a particular port; obtain CBP crossing-level data.

Oct 30 filing · Houston / refining

Valero's $320.5M former Houston refinery deal remains an industrial lead

What happened: Valero agreed to buy the former LyondellBasell Houston refinery property for $320.5M and described an initial restart target of around 165,000 barrels daily of light-crude capacity by late 2027.

Why it matters: Refinery rehabilitation could generate electrical, piping, civil, tank, controls and inspection work around the Houston Ship Channel.

Who may benefit or be hurt: Qualified refinery contractors and trades may benefit; competitors and owners face capital, safety and permitting risk.

Business move: Prepare safety statistics, plant credentials and turnaround references; monitor Valero supplier procurement and permits.
Risks / verification: Closing and restart plans are forward-looking, and no bid package or guaranteed vendor award is implied.

Nov 2 report · Port Arthur / LNG

Golden Pass LNG shipped four October cargoes, up from two in September

What happened: November 2 LNG shipping-data reporting indicated Golden Pass in Southeast Texas exported four cargoes in October versus two in September, amid strong U.S. LNG flows.

Why it matters: More activity can sustain Sabine Pass/Port Arthur marine, terminal-service, instrumentation and equipment-maintenance demand.

Who may benefit or be hurt: Qualified marine and industrial providers may gain; gas-consuming businesses can face fuel-cost exposure.

Business move: Check vendor qualification, shutdown calendars, tug and port demand, and the facility's real procurement needs.
Risks / verification: Cargo counts are shipping-data estimates and do not prove an expansion, contract availability or long-term growth.

Sep 30 groundbreaking · Brownsville jobs

Saronic's Port Alpha shipyard creates a long-cycle workforce target

What happened: Texas officials marked groundbreaking September 30 for Saronic's planned $3B-plus Brownsville shipyard for autonomous and crewed vessel production, with officials projecting up to 10,000 jobs over time.

Why it matters: Major shipbuilding campuses can expand welding, steel fabrication, controls, industrial construction and logistics demand in South Texas.

Who may benefit or be hurt: Training providers, local metal trades and suppliers may benefit; stretched labor markets and unqualified bidders may be disadvantaged.

Business move: Develop a Brownsville subcontractor watchlist and align apprenticeship pathways with documented vendor and safety requirements.
Risks / verification: Job totals and economic-impact figures are projections, not hiring notices or awarded contracts; verify phase and procurement status.

Nov 2 · Brookshire industrial FTZ

Tesla gains limited FTZ production approval for battery-storage work

What happened: An official notice published November 2 confirms FTZ Board approval for specified battery-storage production within Tesla's Brookshire subzone, with a 14-month limit.

Why it matters: Battery manufacturers and suppliers around Houston may have new customs planning work, subject to precise approval scope.

Who may benefit or be hurt: Trade-compliance vendors and suppliers may benefit; importers overestimating duty savings face financial exposure.

Business move: Prepare an FTZ compliance checklist, classify every component and model applicable trade remedies with a customs broker.
Risks / verification: Not a blanket exemption or a new construction contract; verify effective duration and exact authorized products.

Nov 6 deadline · Defense infrastructure

Texas DEAAG infrastructure grant applications close tomorrow

What happened: Texas Military Preparedness Commission states FY2027 Defense Economic Adjustment Assistance Grant applications must arrive by 5 p.m. Central Friday November 6; eligible public entities may seek $50,000 to $5M for qualifying defense-community infrastructure.

Why it matters: Successful public projects can support utility, road, resilience and installation-adjacent construction activity.

Who may benefit or be hurt: Municipalities, counties and certain public districts may qualify; private construction businesses generally cannot apply directly.

Business move: Help an eligible government partner finish scope, match, community/military benefit and a 50-page-or-less application before the deadline.
Risks / verification: Late applications are ineligible; public-entity status and scoring matter; grants are not automatically awarded to a contractor.

Nov 28 / Dec 1 · Texas incentives

Opportunity Zone certifications and Enterprise Zone nominations near

What happened: Texas submitted 608 proposed OZ 2.0 tracts in 106 counties for federal certification expected around November 28; its Enterprise Zone Program local-government nomination deadline is Tuesday December 1.

Why it matters: Rural development, industrial site selection and qualifying Texas employment projects may benefit from tax frameworks when properly structured.

Who may benefit or be hurt: Qualifying investors and municipalities may gain; sites in noncertified tracts or employers missing jobs criteria may not.

Business move: Cross-check parcels against final Treasury maps, ask local economic-development offices about Enterprise Zone nomination and obtain specialist tax review.
Risks / verification: Nomination is not designation; OZ 1.0 overlaps under separate rules. Enterprise Zone refunds require award and job/investment eligibility.
