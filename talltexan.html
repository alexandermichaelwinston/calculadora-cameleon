1 · Statewide · Data Centers

Texas freezes new state-issued data-center permits pending ERCOT’s audit

What happened: Governor Greg Abbott barred TCEQ from issuing new data-center permits until regulators complete the audit of large loads seeking ERCOT interconnection. Reuters reports more than 470 GW of data centers and other large users were in the queue.

Why it matters: This changes the economics of powered land, site control and speculative data-center development. Grid-upgrade responsibility, water, permit status and audit evidence now matter before construction mobilization.

Who may benefit / hurt: Grid-efficiency, water-reuse, power-quality, compliance and behind-the-meter service firms may gain. Landowners and developers counting on fast permitting or incentive-driven valuations may face delays.

Operator move: Require written interconnection status, ERCOT audit status, TCEQ permit path, water supply, transformer/substation scope and cost-allocation evidence before paying a powered-land premium.
Risk check: Project impacts vary by permit and stage. Future incentive changes are not automatic and would require separate action.

2 · Encinal · Power + AI

The 6.3-GW Encinal project becomes South Korea’s first selected U.S. investment-pact project

What happened: South Korea confirmed a Texas gas-fired power project under its $350 billion U.S. investment pact. Reuters says media reports value the Encinal project above $20 billion and size it at 6.3 GW, with power intended for AI data centers and semiconductor factories.

Why it matters: A project of this scale could create years of turbine, pipeline, high-voltage, controls, cooling, water and heavy-civil work in South Texas.

Who may benefit / hurt: Texas EPC, gas, electrical, fabrication, construction and water-system suppliers may benefit. Competing generation projects may encounter tighter equipment, labor and grid capacity.

Operator move: Prepare pre-FID capability packages for gas supply, high-voltage electrical, water, cooling, civil, commissioning and maintenance scopes; identify Korean OEM/EPC relationships.
Risk check: The project needs U.S. investment-committee approval and further negotiations. It is not yet a final construction notice or guaranteed award.
Reuters · Sep. 22
3 · Houston · Pharma Manufacturing

Lilly breaks ground on its $6.5B Houston API plant

What happened: Eli Lilly broke ground September 21 on a roughly 1-million-square-foot active pharmaceutical ingredient manufacturing site at Generation Park in Harris County. Texas says the project represents more than $6.5 billion of investment and more than 600 high-paying jobs; it has a $5.5 million Texas Enterprise Fund grant and JETI approval.

Why it matters: The project deepens domestic pharmaceutical manufacturing and creates a large local market for process equipment, clean utilities, validation, automation, electrical and specialty construction.

Who may benefit / hurt: Houston-area industrial contractors, life-science suppliers, controls firms, water/clean-utility providers and workforce trainers may gain. Competitors for skilled technical labor may face wage pressure.

Operator move: Target vendor registration and qualification for process piping, electrical, instrumentation, controls, cleanrooms, water systems, commissioning, QA/validation and maintenance.
Risk check: Headline capex and jobs do not equal immediately available contracts. Procurement timing, package ownership and qualification requirements must be verified.
Texas Governor · Sep. 21
4 · Laredo · Border Logistics

Pathways for Trade is in session today — turn the symposium into a procurement map

What happened: The September 22–24 Pathways for Trade symposium is underway in Laredo, bringing manufacturers, logistics firms, infrastructure leaders and policymakers together around North American trade. The Night of Trade Ambassador is September 23.

Why it matters: Laredo’s road, rail, warehousing, customs and workforce capacity directly affects U.S.–Mexico freight velocity and nearshoring growth.

Who may benefit / hurt: Carriers, customs brokers, industrial developers, warehouse operators, civil contractors, customs-tech providers and training organizations may benefit.

Operator move: Track every announced infrastructure need, warehouse expansion, rail/customs bottleneck and public procurement date; follow up with the owning agency or company within 48 hours.
Risk check: Panels and networking are not contract awards. Confirm funding, scope, procurement method and bid dates.

5 · Texas ↔ Mexico · USMCA Sourcing

Mexico’s plan to buy more U.S. goods is a direct sales lead for Texas manufacturers

What happened: President Claudia Sheinbaum said Mexico plans to buy more goods from the United States and reduce imports from other countries during the USMCA review.

Why it matters: Texas has a natural logistics advantage into northern Mexico and major border crossings. The policy signal could support machinery, chemicals, fabricated products, food/ag inputs and industrial components made in Texas.

Who may benefit / hurt: Texas exporters, distributors, contract manufacturers and border warehouses may gain. Suppliers dependent on non-regional content may face greater competition or rules-of-origin scrutiny.

Operator move: Create a ‘replace-the-import’ campaign: identify Mexico’s non-U.S. inputs in your category, quote delivered-to-border or delivered-to-plant pricing, and document North American origin.
Risk check: This is not a mandatory buy-American rule for Mexico. Validate demand, classification and final USMCA policy before adding capacity.
Reuters · Sep. 21
6 · Border Finance · Peso

FIX at 17.3015 changes cross-border margin math

What happened: Banco de México’s September 22 FIX rose to 17.3015 MXN/USD from 17.2203 the prior business day, a roughly 0.47% weakening of the peso against the dollar.

Why it matters: Texas exporters paid in dollars may become more expensive to Mexican buyers, while U.S. operators paying peso-denominated Mexican labor or services can see a modest dollar-cost benefit.

Who may benefit / hurt: Dollar buyers of Mexican services may benefit; Mexican importers of Texas equipment and businesses with dollar debt can be hurt.

Operator move: Add FX adjustment bands to Texas–Mexico quotes, shorten validity on equipment bids and match peso costs with peso revenue where possible.
Risk check: FIX is a reference rate, not a live execution quote. Actual bank spreads and payment timing matter.
Banco de México
7 · West Texas · Natural Gas

West Texas Express open season closes Friday at 4 p.m. CST

What happened: Enbridge’s non-binding open season for the proposed West Texas Express pipeline runs through September 25 at 4 p.m. CST. The greenfield intrastate project would connect Waha-area supply with greater El Paso, Hudspeth County and one or more U.S.–Mexico border delivery meters.

Why it matters: New takeaway could support Permian gas, West Texas power demand and Mexico exports while creating construction and service opportunities along the corridor.

Who may benefit / hurt: Compression, valve, metering, electrical, ROW, trenching, civil, inspection and border-meter contractors may gain if the project advances. Competing pipelines face commercial pressure.

Operator move: Map likely contractor packages, equipment needs, staging yards and border-delivery infrastructure now; shippers considering capacity need to meet the open-season deadline.
Risk check: The open season is market testing, not a final investment decision. Route, capacity, permits and commercial support can change.

8 · Austin + Killeen · Foreign-Trade Zones

Texas semiconductor FTZ deadlines: Austin October 5, Killeen October 20

What happened: The U.S. FTZ Board lists Ichor Systems’ Austin production case with comments due October 5 and Dongjin Semichem Texas’ Killeen production case with comments due October 20.

Why it matters: Semiconductor equipment and specialty chemicals use high-value imported inputs where duty deferral, re-export treatment and inventory timing can affect working capital and landed cost.

Who may benefit / hurt: Customs specialists, bonded logistics, semiconductor suppliers and import-intensive manufacturers may benefit. Companies with weak HTS/origin controls risk penalties or nonexistent savings.

Operator move: Offer SKU-level HTS/origin/duty/dwell/re-export analysis, FTZ feasibility, bonded inventory controls and specialty logistics before the comment windows close.
Risk check: FTZ status is not an automatic duty exemption. Section 232/301 and foreign-status rules can materially alter the economics.
U.S. FTZ Board
9 · Statewide · OZ 2.0 + JETI

Texas’ 605 OZ 2.0 nominations can add another 25% JETI value limitation

What happened: Texas nominated 605 census tracts in 105 counties for Opportunity Zone 2.0, with federal certification expected by November 28. Under JETI, qualifying projects in qualified Opportunity Zones are eligible for an additional 25% limitation on taxable value beyond the program’s standard 50% school-district M&O limitation.

Why it matters: For qualifying capital-intensive manufacturing, dispatchable generation, high-tech infrastructure and critical infrastructure projects, the combined location/incentive structure can materially change after-tax economics.

Who may benefit / hurt: Well-sited qualifying projects can benefit. Landowners selling weak sites solely on incentive labels risk disappointing buyers.

Operator move: Overlay OZ nominations with JETI eligibility, power, water, labor, rail/highway, flood risk, FTZ coverage and local abatements before choosing land.
Risk check: OZ certification and JETI approval are separate. Renewable energy and energy-storage projects are not JETI-eligible under the cited program rules.

10 · Statewide · Grants + Tax Refunds

Two Q4 incentive clocks are running: DEAAG November 6 and Enterprise Zone December 1

What happened: Texas’ FY27 Defense Economic Adjustment Assistance Grant applications are due November 6, with awards from $50,000 to $5 million per project. The next Texas Enterprise Zone application deadline is December 1.

Why it matters: Defense-community infrastructure grants and Enterprise Zone sales/use-tax refund designations can improve project economics, but both require organized local sponsors and documentation.

Who may benefit / hurt: Eligible defense communities, infrastructure projects and qualifying companies nominated by Texas communities may benefit. Late or incomplete applicants receive no value from the programs.

Operator move: Start sponsor coordination, project narratives, job/capex evidence, matching-fund documentation and local resolutions now rather than treating the deadline as the start date.
Risk check: Programs are competitive or performance-based and have specific eligibility rules. An application is not an award.
