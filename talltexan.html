1 · San Antonio · Power Equipment

IEM creates a 3,000-job supplier hunt

What: Industrial Electric Mfg. is establishing a $200 million advanced-manufacturing facility at Brooks in San Antonio, with up to 3,000 jobs. Texas extended a $3.36 million Texas Enterprise Fund grant tied to 448 jobs.

Texas impact: Switchgear and power-distribution demand is rising alongside data centers, factories and grid projects, creating openings for metal fabrication, copper, wiring, testing, controls and MRO.

Operator move: build a vendor map around enclosures, busbar, wire harnesses, logistics, industrial maintenance and technical staffing.
“Up to” employment numbers may phase over years; underwrite to actual contracts and hiring milestones.
Texas Governor · Aug. 25
2 · Orange · Workforce

A $489,624 training grant targets 241 industrial workers

What: Texas announced a Skills Development Fund grant to Lamar State College Orange with Arlanxeo, Chevron Phillips Chemical and Indorama Ventures for customized training of 241 new and current workers.

Texas impact: Fire/safety, machining, chemical engineering, lab and nursing skills support Southeast Texas industrial growth and can reduce one of the biggest constraints on new manufacturing: qualified labor.

Operator move: ask local colleges and workforce boards whether your hiring plan can be bundled into an employer-led training application.
Training grants are tied to approved programs, employers and occupations; do not assume reimbursement for existing training costs.
Texas Governor · Aug. 27
3 · Grimes County · Semiconductors

Terafab turns Southeast Texas into a tier-2 / tier-3 supplier map

What: SpaceX plans a vertically integrated semiconductor fabrication plant in Grimes County. The first phase represents more than $16.8 billion of investment and 3,000 jobs, supported by a $30 million TEF grant and JETI qualification.

Texas impact: Cleanrooms, power, water, specialty gases, automation, construction, packaging, industrial real estate and housing all become adjacent markets.

Operator move: identify fab suppliers that need a Texas satellite location within a few hours of Grimes County.
Later phases and total buildout are not guaranteed; qualify opportunities by awarded scope.
Texas Governor · Aug. 6
4 · Houston · Biopharma

Bristol Myers Squibb puts $2.3B behind Houston manufacturing

What: Bristol Myers Squibb is establishing a new Houston pharmaceutical manufacturing campus with about $2.3 billion of capital investment and nearly 500 new jobs. Texas extended a $4.89 million TEF grant and the project also qualifies under JETI.

Texas impact: Houston gains a major anchor for life-science construction, validation, process piping, industrial HVAC, utilities and specialty logistics.

Operator move: prequalify for GMP-adjacent construction, facility services, specialty mechanical and validation work before bid packages peak.
Pharma vendor qualification is stringent; generic commercial credentials may not satisfy quality requirements.
Texas Governor · Aug. 10
5 · Austin · Robotics

Amazon adds a multi-billion-dollar robotics manufacturing node

What: Texas says Amazon selected Austin for a new robotics manufacturing facility expected to create about 300–500 manufacturing and engineering jobs.

Texas impact: The bigger opportunity is the supplier ecosystem around motion control, machine vision, sensors, machining, electronics, software and industrial automation.

Operator move: map Central Texas precision-manufacturing capacity and pitch gaps Amazon’s local supplier base may need filled.
Final supplier awards and commissioning scope can change as the project advances.
Texas Governor · Aug. 19
6 · East Texas · Natural Gas

Haynesville deal talks reinforce LNG + AI gas demand

What: Reuters reports Gunvor is negotiating to buy Haynesville assets in East Texas and Louisiana for roughly $1.2–$1.5 billion. The assets produce about 370 million cubic feet equivalent per day.

Texas impact: AI data centers and Gulf Coast LNG demand can support drilling, gathering, compression, pipeline and field-service activity.

Operator move: track acreage, takeaway capacity and compression needs rather than only gas price — service demand often appears first.
The transaction is still under discussion and may not close.
Reuters · Aug. 25
7 · Border Logistics · Pharr + Laredo

Texas is still adding border-crossing capacity

What: Pharr secured $3.52 million for its BOLT modernization project, including CBP operations, traffic flow and inspection coordination. Laredo is separately advancing an expansion concept that would add an eight-lane northbound bridge beside the existing World Trade Bridge.

Texas impact: Better throughput supports brokers, warehouses, cold storage, trucking, produce, auto parts and cross-border manufacturing — and increases demand for industrial land near crossings.

Operator move: map warehouse and transload sites within 10–20 minutes of the highest-growth crossings before capacity improvements are fully priced into land.
Infrastructure schedules, permitting and border staffing can delay realized throughput gains.

8 · Brownsville · Shipbuilding

Port Alpha is a $3.2B Rio Grande Valley supplier opportunity

What: Saronic Technologies selected the Port of Brownsville for Port Alpha, a next-generation shipyard expected to represent about $3.2 billion of investment and more than 10,000 direct jobs, with construction slated to begin in 2026.

Texas impact: Marine construction, steel, coatings, electrical systems, welding, logistics, housing and workforce services can all expand around the project.

Operator move: build a vendor-readiness checklist now — safety programs, welding certifications, bonding, QA/QC and maritime procurement contacts.
Large projects phase over years; headline economic-impact projections are not guaranteed cash flow for any supplier.
Port of Brownsville · Jul. 16
9 · Incentives · Enterprise Zone

September 1 is an Enterprise Zone application checkpoint

What: Texas’ Enterprise Zone Program provides state sales-and-use tax refunds for qualifying job creation and investment. Quarterly applications are due on the first business day of March, June, September and December.

Texas impact: Companies expanding now can miss meaningful refunds simply by engaging a city or county too late; the community must nominate the project.

Operator move: if today’s round is missed, build the nomination package now for the December deadline and align hiring, wages and capex documentation.
Only qualifying expenditures and jobs count; communities have limited designations and the company must meet program thresholds.
Texas Enterprise Zone Program
10 · Opportunity Zones + JETI

Texas OZ 2.0 moves from local nominations to certification watch

What: Texas’ local Opportunity Zone 2.0 nomination process is closed and the state said it intended to submit tracts to Treasury by Aug. 17. JETI provides qualifying projects a 10-year school-district M&O value limitation, with an additional 25% limitation for projects in qualified Opportunity Zones.

Texas impact: The strongest industrial sites can combine federal capital-gains incentives with JETI, local abatements, workforce tools and potentially FTZ economics.

Operator move: build a site matrix now around likely/final OZ status, JETI thresholds, substation capacity, water, rail/highway access and labor.
Do not underwrite OZ benefits until Treasury certification; JETI also requires county-specific job and investment thresholds plus approvals.
