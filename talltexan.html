NOV 23–24 · BRAZOS COUNTY / JETI

TSMC proposes $22.6B fab and at least 2,099 direct jobs

What happened: Brazos County says TSMC is evaluating a College Station-area site for a 2nm-or-better chip fab. The application projects at least 2,099 direct jobs and average annual pay of $133,000; Reuters identifies Arizona as an alternative.

Why it matters: This could create a major market for cleanroom construction, electrical systems, specialty utilities and technical labor.

Who may benefit or be hurt: Industrial contractors, equipment suppliers and technical colleges may benefit; local water, power, housing and competing employers could face pressure.

Actionable business idea: Prepare a semiconductor-grade safety, quality and bonding package; track College Station ISD, county and state JETI proceedings.
Risks / verification: This is a site evaluation, not a final project commitment or job advertisement. Verify tax approval, utility capacity, construction schedule and bid notices.
Brazos County · Nov 23 ↗Reuters · Nov 24 ↗
NOV 24 · DALLAS / INDUSTRIAL REAL ESTATE

Dallas industrial rents rise 7.7% year over year

What happened: CommercialCafe’s November 24 report cites Yardi data showing 7.7% annual growth in Dallas in-place rents through October; Dallas industrial sales reached roughly $6B year to date.

Why it matters: Nearshoring and distribution demand can favor modern warehouses and tenant improvements, but higher occupancy costs constrain margins.

Who may benefit or be hurt: Warehouse owners, racking contractors and logistics providers may benefit; small tenants and speculative developers face rent and financing risk.

Actionable business idea: Pursue tenant improvement, dock retrofit and power-upgrade work; underwrite leases using signed comparables and realistic concessions.
Risks / verification: Reported sales are property transactions, not new construction spending. Verify vacancy, local rent terms and tenant credit.
CommercialCafe / Yardi · Nov 24 ↗
NOV 24 · TEXAS FABRICATION / NATIONAL MARKET

$22B national fabricated-metal project pipeline offers Texas supplier leads

What happened: Industrial Info Resources reports roughly $22B in active and proposed U.S. fabricated-metal capital projects spanning power, AI infrastructure, defense and automotive.

Why it matters: Texas machine shops and steel fabricators could compete for electrical infrastructure and industrial equipment orders nationwide.

Who may benefit or be hurt: Qualified fabricators, inspectors and welders may benefit; smaller shops face working-capital and certification costs.

Actionable business idea: Build a bid-ready capability sheet covering tolerances, welding qualifications, capacity, lead times and freight lanes.
Risks / verification: The $22B is a national private-research pipeline, not Texas spending or awarded contracts. Validate project-by-project procurement and credit.
Industrial Info Resources · Nov 24 ↗
NOV 20 RELEASE · TEXAS JOBS

Texas adds 30,600 jobs; construction grows 6,900 and manufacturing 5,200

What happened: TWC's October 2026 survey reported 14,500,500 nonfarm jobs, +30,600 from September; construction added 6,900 jobs and manufacturing added 5,200. The seasonally adjusted unemployment rate remained 4.4%.

Why it matters: The detailed sector splits can guide Texas contractor staffing and industrial sales rather than relying on broad job-creation pledges.

Who may benefit or be hurt: Skilled trades, training centers and industrial suppliers may gain; smaller builders can face rising wage pressure.

Actionable business idea: Update 2027 bid labor rates, partner with workforce boards, and target high-demand manufacturing maintenance occupations.
Risks / verification: Preliminary statewide net changes are not open job postings; regional demand can diverge and estimates may be revised.
Texas Workforce Commission · labor data ↗TWC release via workforce board · Nov 20 ↗
NOV 19 · SABINE PASS / ORANGE–BEAUMONT

Sabine Pass LNG approval creates a Southeast Texas pursuit list

What happened: FERC authorized three new Sabine Pass liquefaction trains in nearby Cameron Parish, Louisiana, plus pipeline-related works, a major regulatory step for the Gulf Coast.

Why it matters: Orange, Beaumont and Port Arthur shops can competitively service nearby plants from Texas yards and crews.

Who may benefit or be hurt: Industrial electricians, welders, instrument technicians and crane providers may benefit; local roads and labor markets may face congestion.

Actionable business idea: Identify qualified Cheniere-tier subcontractors and prequalify for pipe spools, instrumentation, safety and commissioning packages.
Risks / verification: FERC approval is not a final investment decision or vendor award; verify schedule, emissions conditions, workforce needs and gate qualifications.
FERC · Nov 19 ↗
NOV 13 DATA · LAREDO / EL PASO

Border truck corridor handles $75.4B of U.S.–Mexico freight

What happened: BTS's September release put U.S.–Mexico freight at $96.3B, of which $75.4B moved by truck. It named Laredo, El Paso and Otay Mesa as leading Mexico truck border ports.

Why it matters: Texas land crossings remain essential to nearshoring and industrial inventory flow, increasing demand for secure yards and customs services.

Who may benefit or be hurt: Laredo customs brokers, drayage firms and industrial landlords may gain; small importers face detention and compliance exposure.

Actionable business idea: Price cross-dock and trailer parking by dwell time, and test a customs-document quality assurance service for manufacturers.
Risks / verification: September value is not November truck count; confirm crossing-specific capacity, rates and zoning before acquiring property.
U.S. BTS · Sep 2026 freight ↗
NOV 24 CHECK · GARWOOD / WILMER / HARLINGEN

Three Texas foreign-trade zone comment periods remain listed

What happened: The FTZ Board lists Garwood production (Dec 7), Samsung Electro-Mechanics Wilmer subzone (Dec 14) and Penn Aluminum Harlingen production (Dec 23).

Why it matters: These cases provide concrete customs and supplier-compliance leads for utility, electronics and aluminum industries.

Who may benefit or be hurt: Zone operators and customs specialists may gain; competing suppliers may face changes in costs.

Actionable business idea: Contact zone grantees to assess compliant storage and production needs; track individual notices and public comments.
Risks / verification: Pending cases are not approvals or automatic exemptions. Check the Federal Register for official dates, authorized products and scope.
U.S. Commerce · current open cases ↗
DEC 1 DEADLINE · TEXAS EZP

Local governments have a December 1 Enterprise Zone nomination deadline

What happened: Texas's Enterprise Zone Program shows its FY2027 Q2 nomination deadline as Tuesday, December 1, 2026. Communities, not firms acting alone, must nominate eligible projects for a possible state sales/use tax refund.

Why it matters: The program may lower effective investment cost for qualified facilities creating or retaining jobs.

Who may benefit or be hurt: Designated firms and local economic developers may gain; projects missing job or investment thresholds will not qualify.

Actionable business idea: Have the city or county economic-development office screen investment, hiring and local nomination paperwork now.
Risks / verification: No automatic rebate: location, jobs, qualifying spend, nomination fee and application timing matter. The program is competitive and contingent on designation.
Texas Enterprise Zone portal · official deadlines ↗
JAN 1 EFFECTIVE · TEXAS OPPORTUNITY ZONES

Texas's 608 new Opportunity Zone tracts take effect January 1

What happened: Texas says the U.S. Treasury designated 608 tracts in 106 Texas counties on November 5, 2026, effective January 1, 2027, for the refreshed federal Qualified Opportunity Zone program.

Why it matters: A new eligible geography can reshape after-tax feasibility for qualified industrial, housing and commercial redevelopment.

Who may benefit or be hurt: Qualified fund investors and development partners may benefit; sites outside certified tracts or investments that fail tax rules may receive no advantage.

Actionable business idea: Overlay the official tract map on proposed warehouse and infill redevelopment acquisitions; engage tax counsel before drafting fund terms.
Risks / verification: Tax benefits depend on qualifying structures, gains, holding periods and final regulations; designation alone does not subsidize projects.
Texas Governor · OZ 2.0 designations ↗
MAR 12 GRANT WINDOW · TEXAS SEAPORTS

TCEQ offers port and rail-yard fleet replacement grants

What happened: TCEQ's $24M initial Seaport and Rail Yard Areas Emissions Reduction Program funding can cover up to 80% of eligible drayage-truck and cargo-equipment replacement or repower costs; applications close March 12, 2027 at 5 p.m. Central, unless funds run out.

Why it matters: Upgrading older cargo equipment can improve air quality and reduce downtime at qualifying Texas logistics hubs.

Who may benefit or be hurt: Eligible fleet owners and equipment dealers may benefit; operators outside designated areas or without requisite operating history may not qualify.

Actionable business idea: Audit two years of equipment use and title/lease history, price replacements, and submit early through the designated portal.
Risks / verification: First-come program; equipment generally needs at least 200 days/year eligible operations for the past two years. Confirm counties, equipment eligibility and remaining funding.
