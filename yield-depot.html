The October 2026 readout

Bond yields fell. The Fed did not.

The Federal Reserve held its policy range at 3.75%–4.00% on October 28 by a 10–2 vote; the two dissenters wanted a 25-basis-point increase. Yet yields fell across most of the Treasury curve during October, creating price gains for existing bondholders while slightly reducing the yields available to fresh buyers. [1–3]

Premium Alpha / Cash desk

1. Cash still buys time

The 3-month Treasury benchmark stood at 4.16% on October 30. SGOV's 3.79% SEC yield on October 29 is lower because an ETF's net income calculation reflects its portfolio's holdings and expenses, not one point on the Treasury par curve. A staggered Treasury-bill ladder may lock in specific maturities; a T-bill ETF emphasizes convenience and rolling exposure. Neither is identical to an FDIC-insured bank account. [2, 4]

Catalyst

Fed guidance on future short rates; tighter policy could help new bill yields while older holdings roll off.

Risk

Reinvestment yield can fall quickly once monetary policy eases. Fund share prices can move modestly.

Premium Alpha / Tax-aware income

2. Munis reward careful tax math

In Franklin Templeton's October 23 municipal monitor, the Bloomberg AAA benchmark curve showed 3.05% at five years, 3.52% at ten, and 4.85% at thirty. The 10-year AAA yield fell 22 basis points in the week to October 23 as municipals rallied. These are benchmark curve yields; a bond available to purchase may differ materially. [5]

Tax-equivalent-yield laboratory

For a federally tax-exempt yield, TEY = tax-exempt yield ÷ (1 − assumed marginal federal tax rate). Illustrative federal-only scenarios, excluding NIIT, AMT, state/local tax differences and transaction costs:

Break-even insight:

At a simplified 25% federal marginal rate, SUB's 3.12% tax-exempt SEC yield equals a 4.16% taxable benchmark before accounting for their different durations and methodologies. For the 10-year comparison on the same October 23 observation date, 3.52% AAA municipal vs. 5.00% Treasury has a federal tax break-even of 29.6%. Neither comparison proves superiority because maturities, call features, credit and liquidity differ.

SUB's latest reported effective duration was 1.97 years, compared with far greater duration exposure in long individual municipal bonds. Federal tax exemption is not universal across every security and investor, and state/local taxation varies. A long muni's very high TEY should never obscure the price losses that a renewed rate jump could cause. [5–6]

Premium Alpha / Credit desk

3. Extra credit yield has a price

ICSH reported a 4.40% SEC yield on October 26, with 0.62-year effective duration and about 30 basis points of option-adjusted spread on October 27. That is a relatively modest credit spread for taking issuer and liquidity risk. The portfolio is designed for ultra-short investment-grade exposure, not guaranteed principal. [7]

Decision rule:

Compare securities at similar maturities and after fees, taxes and liquidity needs. Do not compare a 4.40% credit ETF with a Treasury quote at a different duration and call the difference a free pickup. High-yield credit belongs in a separate risk category, not in the cash bucket.

Premium Alpha / Dividend Hunter™

4. Quality dividends must earn the equity premium

SCHD reported 3.09% 30-day SEC yield as of October 29, versus a 3.24% trailing distribution yield as of September 30. Its issuer reported a portfolio P/E of 18.24×, price/cash-flow of 10.68× and return on equity of 30.11% as of September 30. These metrics suggest a comparatively value-oriented exposure; they are not proof that every underlying company's dividend is safe. [8]

Dividend durability checklist

For each underlying company, examine free-cash-flow coverage of dividends, payout ratio, net debt and refinancing ladder, earnings cyclicality, capital-spending requirements, and years of consecutive payment growth. SCHD's index emphasizes dividend sustainability and financial strength, but issuer-level screening is still necessary. Its ten largest holdings represented roughly 42% of assets on October 29, an additional concentration factor. [8–10]

Relative value: SCHD's 3.09% SEC yield is below the 4.16% 3-month Treasury benchmark. The equity case depends on possible long-run dividend growth and capital appreciation, with meaningful potential losses—not current yield alone.
