1. Cash & Money-Market Watch

Yield Depot compares government money-market funds, Treasury funds, high-yield savings and ultra-short bond choices. A money-market fund is an investment fund, not an FDIC-insured bank deposit. Rates change frequently, so verify current yield, fees, minimums and tax treatment before acting.

Government money-market funds

TAX ANGLE

Treasury-heavy cash

Treasury interest can receive favorable state/local tax treatment. Compare the after-tax result, not only nominal APY.

LIQUIDITY

Ultra-short bonds

Potentially more yield, but NAV can fluctuate. Do not treat an ultra-short bond ETF as identical to insured cash.

2. Municipal Bonds: What You Keep

Municipal interest is generally exempt from federal income tax, and qualifying in-state bonds can also receive state/local tax advantages. That makes the tax-equivalent yield important: tax-exempt yield ÷ (1 − marginal tax rate). For example, a 3.7% tax-exempt yield is roughly 5.87% taxable-equivalent at a 37% federal marginal rate, before state taxes.
