1 · Mexico · USMCA Sourcing

Mexico plans to buy more U.S. goods and reduce imports from other countries

What happened: President Claudia Sheinbaum said Mexico is planning to increase purchases from the United States and reduce imports from other countries as part of the USMCA review. She said Washington is asking Mexico to reduce the U.S. trade deficit within the agreement.

Why it matters: The signal points toward deliberate North American sourcing substitution, not just tariff negotiation. U.S. suppliers could gain share in machinery, components, chemicals, agricultural goods and industrial inputs that Mexico currently sources elsewhere.

Who may benefit / hurt: U.S. exporters, Mexican distributors and North American suppliers with traceable regional content may gain. Asian and other non-regional suppliers serving Mexican factories could lose orders or face stronger localization pressure.

Action: Identify the top non-U.S. inputs entering your Mexican operation or customer base and build a U.S.-source substitution list with landed-cost, lead-time and USMCA-origin comparisons.
Risk check: Sheinbaum described a policy direction, not a published procurement mandate. Product-level changes, timing and tariff treatment still need official rules.
Reuters · Sep. 21
2 · Texas · Energy + AI Infrastructure

South Korea confirms the 6.3-GW Encinal project as its first U.S. investment-pact project

What happened: South Korea confirmed a gas-fired power plant in Encinal, Texas as the first project selected under its $350 billion U.S. investment pact. Reuters reports the project at 6.3 GW and more than $20 billion, intended to supply electricity to AI data centers and chip factories.

Why it matters: If implemented at that scale, it would be one of the largest new power-and-industrial construction opportunities in North America, with demand for turbines, pipelines, substations, transmission, switchgear, cooling, water, controls and heavy civil work.

Who may benefit / hurt: Texas EPC, gas, electrical, water, construction and equipment suppliers could gain. Competing power projects may face labor, equipment, transmission and gas-capacity competition.

Action: Build prequalification packages for generation, pipeline, electrical, water and industrial-civil scopes; map Korean OEM and contractor relationships before formal procurement starts.
Risk check: The project still requires U.S. investment-committee approval and further U.S.–Korea negotiation. Project size, ownership and timing can change before final announcement or FID.
Reuters · Sep. 22
3 · Mexico · Korea Nearshoring

South Korea’s president arrives in Mexico this week for a Sheinbaum summit and MOUs

What happened: President Lee Jae Myung is scheduled to arrive in Mexico City Wednesday and meet President Sheinbaum Thursday. The two governments plan a joint press conference and memorandum signings, with South Korea seeking cooperation in trade, energy, aerospace, culture and defense.

Why it matters: Korean manufacturers are already embedded in Mexico’s auto, electronics and industrial base. Government-level agreements can accelerate supplier localization, energy infrastructure and aerospace investment.

Who may benefit / hurt: Mexican industrial parks, utilities, logistics providers, Korean suppliers and North American component makers may benefit. Existing suppliers unable to meet cost, quality or regional-content targets could lose share.

Action: Mexican states, developers and suppliers should prepare Korean-investor packages that combine power, water, labor, customs access, incentive eligibility and verified supplier capacity.
Risk check: MOUs are frameworks, not factory commitments. Treat named capex, sites and job counts as real only after companies and governments publish them.
Reuters · Sep. 21
4 · Energy · Freight

Oil falls below $100 as Gulf supply improves — but diesel remains the harder cost problem

What happened: Brent fell to about $98.23 Tuesday and actively traded November WTI to about $90.01 as Saudi Arabia restarted its East–West Pipeline and Iran signaled the Strait of Hormuz could reopen within seven days if U.S. pressure eases. At the same time, global diesel prices remain at record highs and U.S. retail diesel has exceeded $6 per gallon.

Why it matters: A crude-price retreat can help plastics, chemicals, asphalt and some freight costs, but diesel-heavy trucking, construction and agriculture may not receive proportional relief.

Who may benefit / hurt: Fuel-intensive manufacturers benefit if crude stays lower; Gulf Coast refiners can still benefit from strong distillate margins. Truck fleets and contractors remain exposed to tight diesel supply.

Action: Model crude and diesel separately. Keep diesel-indexed surcharges and escalation clauses even if headline oil falls below $100.
Risk check: Hormuz traffic remains impaired and diplomacy can reverse quickly. Saudi pipeline operations are restarting from low rates, not instantly returning the system to normal.

5 · Mexico · Dollar/Peso

Latest official FIX moves to 17.2203 MXN/USD

What happened: Banco de México’s official FIX for September 21 is 17.2203 pesos per dollar, compared with 17.2450 on September 18.

Why it matters: A firmer peso raises the dollar-equivalent cost of Mexican payroll and local services while lowering the peso cost of dollar-priced equipment and imported inputs.

Who may benefit / hurt: Mexican equipment importers and companies with dollar purchases may benefit from peso strength; exporters earning dollars but paying peso expenses can see margin pressure.

Action: Quote with explicit FX bands, short validity periods, natural hedges and automatic currency adjustments on cross-border contracts.
Risk check: FIX is an official reference rate determined from wholesale quotes, not a live executable spot price.

6 · Canada · Tariff Shock

Bank of Canada says new U.S. tariffs could cut fourth-quarter growth below 1%

What happened: Bank of Canada Governor Tiff Macklem said the latest U.S. tariff escalation could roughly halve Canada’s fourth-quarter growth to below 1% and again delay investment and hiring. He also said Canadian businesses are already changing supply chains and sourcing strategies to reduce tariff exposure.

Why it matters: North American sourcing is fragmenting by product and country even inside the USMCA framework. That creates substitution opportunities for U.S. and Mexican suppliers.

Who may benefit / hurt: Alternate U.S. and Mexican suppliers may win displaced business. Canadian exporters and U.S. buyers dependent on affected Canadian products face margin and planning risk.

Action: Audit Canadian-origin SKUs and customer exposure before the September 29 U.S. restrictions take effect; prequalify substitute suppliers where HTS exposure is material.
Risk check: Tariffs are product-specific. Verify HTS classification, exemptions and effective dates before changing suppliers or pricing.
Reuters · Sep. 21
7 · Mexico Pacific · Logistics Risk

Category 5 Hurricane Polo puts southwest-Mexico logistics and Manzanillo-area supply chains on watch

What happened: Hurricane Polo strengthened to Category 5 off Mexico’s Pacific coast Tuesday. Its center is forecast to remain offshore, but Guerrero and Michoacán face dangerous flooding and mudslides, while a tropical-storm watch extends toward Manzanillo and rain also threatens Colima and Jalisco.

Why it matters: Manzanillo is a major Pacific gateway for Asian inputs used by Mexican factories. Even without direct landfall, heavy rain, surf, road closures and navigation restrictions can delay containers and inland trucking.

Who may benefit / hurt: Alternate ports, inland warehouses and expedited logistics providers may gain demand. Importers, produce shippers and manufacturers using Pacific routes face delay and inventory risk.

Action: Confirm port and highway status before dispatch, identify alternate inventory release points, and prioritize critical containers and time-sensitive produce.
Risk check: The storm track remains uncertain and a direct Manzanillo shutdown was not established in the sources used here. Verify port authority notices before rerouting cargo.

8 · Border Agriculture · Santa Teresa

Santa Teresa cattle reopening remains conditional — verify the port before routing loads

What happened: USDA’s current screwworm-status page says Douglas, Arizona reopened August 24 and that Santa Teresa and Columbus, New Mexico will be considered for subsequent reopening only after USDA evaluates the initial reopening and Mexico’s progress on agreed safeguards. Earlier industry reporting had pointed to September 24 for Santa Teresa, but USDA’s current public status does not present that date as a guaranteed opening.

Why it matters: Santa Teresa is a major livestock corridor into U.S. feeding and processing networks; uncertainty affects cattle scheduling, hauling, inspections and feedlot procurement.

Who may benefit / hurt: Chihuahua ranchers, livestock haulers, veterinarians and Texas/New Mexico feedlots benefit when trade resumes safely. Animal-health failures or premature routing can create costly delays and renewed restrictions.

Action: Build compliant identification, wound-inspection and traceability processes, but verify USDA port status immediately before dispatch rather than booking around an assumed September 24 reopening.
Risk check: USDA says the timeline is flexible and can change with screwworm risk and Mexico’s progress on the Joint Action Plan.

9 · Texas · Foreign-Trade Zones

Austin and Killeen semiconductor FTZ deadlines create immediate customs work

What happened: The U.S. FTZ Board lists Ichor Systems’ Austin production matter with comments due October 5 and Dongjin Semichem Texas’ Killeen production matter with comments due October 20.

Why it matters: Semiconductor equipment and specialty chemicals contain expensive imported inputs where duty deferral, re-export treatment and inventory timing can materially affect landed cost and working capital.

Who may benefit / hurt: Import-intensive manufacturers, customs specialists and bonded-logistics providers can benefit where savings exceed compliance costs. Weak HTS/origin controls can create penalties or erase savings.

Action: Build SKU-level models covering HTS code, country of origin, duty rate, inventory dwell, re-export share and privileged-foreign-status treatment.
Risk check: Section 232/301 treatment and other special duties can change the apparent benefit. Federal Register notices remain the documents of record.

10 · Texas · Incentive Stack

OZ 2.0, JETI, DEAAG and Enterprise Zone deadlines create a Q4 site-selection window

What happened: Texas nominated 605 census tracts in 105 counties for Opportunity Zone 2.0. JETI provides qualifying projects in certified Opportunity Zones an additional 25% limitation on taxable value. FY27 DEAAG applications close November 6, and the next Texas Enterprise Zone round closes December 1.

Why it matters: Federal capital-gains treatment, school-tax limitations, defense-community grants and sales/use-tax refunds can materially change after-tax project economics when the underlying site has power, water, labor and logistics.

Who may benefit / hurt: Capital-intensive manufacturers, energy projects and defense communities can benefit. Speculative buyers can overpay for sites if they treat incentives as a substitute for infrastructure.

Action: Build one site-selection matrix combining power, water, labor, OZ, JETI, FTZ, Enterprise Zone, DEAAG eligibility, rail/highway access and local abatements.
Risk check: Treasury OZ certification, JETI, Enterprise Zone and DEAAG are separate programs with separate approvals. Texas OZ nominations are not yet final federal certifications.
