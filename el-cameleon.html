Nov 13 data release · U.S.–Mexico freight

North American border freight reaches $161.1B; Mexico corridor gains 34.2%

What happened: The Bureau of Transportation Statistics released September 2026 transborder freight data on November 13: $161.1 billion total across Canada and Mexico, up 23.4% from a year earlier. U.S.–Mexico freight was $96.3 billion, up 34.2%; trucks carried $75.4 billion on the Mexico corridor.

Why it matters: Larger measured trade values sharpen demand forecasts for customs capacity, freight brokerage, crossings, warehouses and security.

Who may benefit or be hurt: Border logistics operators, industrial landlords and compliance firms could gain; shippers may face congestion and higher handling costs.

Actionable business idea: Map top border ports and bid on customs automation, yard space, drayage or cross-dock services.
Risks / verification: September goods values are not November activity or inflation-adjusted volumes. Check freight by commodity and port before expanding.

Nov 13 policy · U.S. coastwise shipping

Jones Act waiver extended 90 days, but each foreign-flagged voyage still needs review

What happened: Reuters reported a 90-day extension of U.S. coastwise shipping waivers for oil and other commodities, retaining case-by-case consideration rather than a blanket exemption.

Why it matters: Qualified routes may gain shipping flexibility as gasoline, diesel and fertilizer distribution costs shift.

Who may benefit or be hurt: Fuel distributors and some charterers may benefit; U.S.-flag ship operators face added competition on authorized voyages.

Actionable business idea: Evaluate coastal fuel routing and compare eligible freight offers against rail and pipeline alternatives.
Risks / verification: Do not assume a voyage is exempt. Confirm the operative waiver, exact cargo, voyage approval and insurance before booking.

Nov 12–13 · USMCA and tariff negotiations

Mexico tariff warning remains a threat while Washington signals trade-talk optimism

What happened: After President Trump threatened to double tariffs on Mexico on November 12, Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent said on November 13 he remained optimistic about deals with Mexico and Canada. No signed deal or implemented tariff increase was announced in that report.

Why it matters: Contractual tariff exposure persists across automotive, steel, agriculture and manufacturing supply chains.

Who may benefit or be hurt: Customs advisers and diversified suppliers may gain planning demand; importers face pricing and sourcing uncertainty.

Actionable business idea: Build landed-cost sensitivity sheets and identify HTS classification, USMCA origin and contract pass-through terms.
Risks / verification: A political statement is not an enacted tariff. Verify CBP notices, presidential documents and effective dates.

Nov 11 · Mexico steel trade enforcement

Mexico reports canceling roughly 1,100 questionable steel-mill registrations

What happened: Mexico's deputy economy minister described tightened steel traceability to curb third-country transshipment and reported approximately 1,100 mill registrations canceled amid U.S. tariff negotiations.

Why it matters: Stricter origin documentation may materially change audit burden for steel, tubing and fabricated-component supply chains.

Who may benefit or be hurt: Verified mills, origin auditors and trade-compliance firms could benefit; inadequately documented traders may be delayed.

Actionable business idea: Offer supplier traceability checks, mill-certificate validation and customs record retention reviews.
Risks / verification: The reported cancellations do not mean every affected shipment is unlawful; seek official Mexican and U.S. customs requirements.

Nov 9 figures · Mexico automotive manufacturing

Mexico produced 302,803 light vehicles in October, with 258,137 exported

What happened: INEGI's November 9 release reported 302,803 vehicles produced and 258,137 exported in October 2026. First-ten-month production was 3,254,062, down 3.9% versus the same period in 2025.

Why it matters: Automotive factory utilization affects parts shipments, specialized manufacturing, rail traffic and border trucking.

Who may benefit or be hurt: Maintenance and aftermarket suppliers may find opportunities, while businesses tied to weaker assembly schedules may lose volume.

Actionable business idea: Diversify supplier revenue into spare parts, industrial maintenance and alternative vehicle-component customers.
Risks / verification: Do not attribute the decline solely to tariffs; assess model cycles, demand, outages and firm-level production plans.

Nov 13 FIX · Mexico finance and purchasing

Peso-dollar planning: latest verified FIX is 18.5525 MXN per USD

What happened: Banco de México's official table lists a November 13 FIX of 18.5525 pesos per dollar; as checked November 16 in the morning, a new November 16 FIX was not yet shown. Banxico's November 5 decision lowered its policy rate to 6.25% effective November 6.

Why it matters: FX and local financing assumptions change landed purchase costs, capital budgets and supplier margins.

Who may benefit or be hurt: Hedged buyers and finance advisers may gain; unhedged importers and exporters may be hurt by rate moves.

Actionable business idea: Quote Mexican supplies in both currencies and stress-test 3–5% exchange-rate scenarios.
Risks / verification: FIX is a reference, not an available retail quote; verify timestamp, settlement date and commercial loan terms.

Nov 13 DOE report · Manufacturing power

Texas industrial nuclear project clears a construction-permit milestone

What happened: The U.S. Department of Energy confirmed the NRC issued a November 9 construction permit for Dow and X-energy's proposed four Xe-100 reactors at Seadrift, Texas, with up to 320 MW of electrical capacity plus industrial steam.

Why it matters: Industrial co-generation can create demand for quality-assured specialty steel, controls, welding and grid services.

Who may benefit or be hurt: Qualified U.S. industrial contractors and vendors could gain; incumbent steam providers face potential competition.

Actionable business idea: Prepare nuclear-quality certifications and monitor owner-approved prequalification and supplier channels.
Risks / verification: A construction permit is not an operating license or confirmed subcontract award. Verify final financing and schedules.

Current open dockets · North American trade zones

Harlingen aluminum and Wilmer electronics FTZ applications open for comments

What happened: The U.S. FTZ Board lists Penn Aluminum International's Harlingen production request (B-143-2026) closing December 23 and Samsung Electro-Mechanics America's Wilmer subzone request (S-479-2026) closing December 14.

Why it matters: Trade-zone decisions can affect manufacturing duty treatment, warehouse processes and investment location choices.

Who may benefit or be hurt: Customs specialists, Texas equipment suppliers and industrial parks may gain; competing producers may seek scrutiny.

Actionable business idea: Review the exact docket, model input/output classifications, and submit documented comments or compliance proposals.
Risks / verification: Applications are pending, not approvals; FTZ admission does not automatically erase Section 232 or other duties.

Nov 16 regional survey · Rio Grande Valley industrialization

South Texas reports more than $110B in announced or underway projects

What happened: A November 15 regional inventory describes over $110 billion in announced or underway Rio Grande Valley projects involving LNG, aerospace, AI, maritime and energy, including Port of Brownsville developments.

Why it matters: A large multi-sector pipeline could strain labor, electricity, water and contractor capacity.

Who may benefit or be hurt: Industrial contractors, workforce trainers and suppliers may gain; residents and small operators can face cost pressure.

Actionable business idea: Build a vetted project pipeline by owner, approval stage, bid portal and construction trade rather than chasing headline totals.
Risks / verification: The reported total aggregates proposals and active investments; it is not approved spending, annual jobs or contracts.

Nov 18 deadline · Public infrastructure grants

Texas opens a $92M hazard-mitigation funding window with two days remaining

What happened: Texas GLO's Mitigation Reallocation Program has $92 million for eligible local-government and district projects addressing flood/drainage, water, wastewater, roads and emergency communications. Project submissions close November 18 at 5 p.m. Central.

Why it matters: The funds could advance resilience infrastructure serving trade corridors and hurricane-exposed communities.

Who may benefit or be hurt: Eligible public entities and later qualified engineering firms may benefit; ineligible private applicants cannot apply directly.

Actionable business idea: Public agencies with existing TIGR access should prioritize complete submissions; contractors can track subsequent procurement.
Risks / verification: TIGR access-request date was November 11. Funding and invitations are competitive; submission is not an award.
