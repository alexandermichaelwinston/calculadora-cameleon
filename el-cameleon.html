1 · U.S.–Mexico · Trade

Mexico says progress continues toward a U.S. trade deal

What happened: President Claudia Sheinbaum said Friday that Mexico is doing everything it can to reach a U.S. trade deal soon after a productive call with President Donald Trump. Mexico continues seeking relief on U.S. tariffs affecting steel, aluminum and cars.

Why it matters: A bilateral package could reset landed costs, auto sourcing and metals economics before the broader USMCA review is resolved.

Who may benefit / hurt: Suppliers with auditable North American content and flexible U.S.–Mexico capacity may gain. Import-heavy assemblers and companies dependent on unsettled tariff treatment remain exposed.

Action: Keep three 2027 pricing models live—current rules, partial tariff relief and stricter regional-content requirements.
Risk check: No final legal text or tariff schedule has been released. Political statements do not change customs treatment by themselves.
Reuters · Sep. 18
2 · Dollar · Peso

Banxico’s latest official FIX rises to 17.2450 MXN/USD

What happened: Banco de México’s September 18 FIX is 17.2450 pesos per U.S. dollar, up from 17.1872 on September 17 and 16.9707 one week earlier.

Why it matters: Peso weakness can reduce the dollar-equivalent cost of Mexican wages and rent while increasing the peso cost of dollar-priced equipment, fuel and imported inputs.

Who may benefit / hurt: Dollar-paid Mexican exporters with peso expenses can benefit. Importers and firms with dollar liabilities face more pressure.

Action: Use FX bands, shorter quote validity, natural hedges and automatic currency-adjustment clauses.
Risk check: FIX is an official reference rate, not a live spot or retail-bank execution quote.

3 · Tariffs · Sanctions

New Russia sanctions law gives Washington broad new tariff power

What happened: A law signed September 18 requires tariffs of up to 100% within 30 days on goods from the five largest importers of Russian oil or gas and certain major sanctions-evasion facilitators, while giving the president broad implementation discretion.

Why it matters: Companies across the Americas can face new indirect tariff and sourcing risk if a supplier country is designated, even when the product itself has nothing to do with energy.

Who may benefit / hurt: U.S., Mexican and other regional substitute suppliers may gain from diverted sourcing. Importers exposed to designated countries could face sudden landed-cost jumps.

Action: Add country-level Russian-energy exposure to supplier risk maps and identify alternate sources for high-value imported SKUs.
Risk check: The law does not yet identify every target country or tariff rate. Do not assume exposure until implementation is published.
Reuters · Sep. 18
4 · Canada · USMCA

September 29 U.S. import bans raise North American sourcing pressure

What happened: U.S. restrictions on a broad group of Canadian alcohol, dairy and motorcycle imports are scheduled to take effect September 29 after U.S.–Canada talks broke down and retaliatory Canadian tariffs took effect.

Why it matters: USMCA-era sourcing can no longer be treated as one uniform North American risk profile; product, origin and destination increasingly matter.

Who may benefit / hurt: U.S. and Mexican substitute suppliers may gain share. Canadian exporters and U.S. buyers dependent on affected goods can lose volume or margin.

Action: Audit Canadian-origin SKUs now and prequalify U.S./Mexico alternatives before September 29.
Risk check: Restrictions are product-specific. Verify HTS classification, exceptions and effective dates before switching suppliers.
Reuters · Sep. 8
5 · Oil · Logistics

Brent closes at $104.87 and WTI at $100.30

What happened: Oil eased Friday after China urged Iran to restrain Houthi attacks on Saudi oil infrastructure, but Brent still settled at $104.87 and WTI at $100.30 as the Middle East supply outlook remained uncertain.

Why it matters: Diesel, freight, asphalt, plastics, chemicals and construction continue carrying a geopolitical cost premium even after crude retreated from weekly highs.

Who may benefit / hurt: Producers, refiners and fuel-logistics providers can benefit. Trucking, manufacturing and fixed-price construction remain exposed.

Action: Keep fuel escalation clauses active and model a sustained-$100 case beside a rapid-normalization case.
Risk check: Oil can move quickly in either direction as shipping routes, attacks and repair timelines change.
Reuters · Sep. 18
6 · USMCA · AI Hardware

AI servers and chips are becoming a rules-of-origin localization play

What happened: U.S. negotiators are pressing Mexico to accept tighter North American origin and traceability rules for AI hardware, including servers and chips, to reduce non-regional content entering the U.S. through Mexico.

Why it matters: Mexico’s fast-growing AI-hardware export base could create new regional demand for racks, power electronics, cooling, cabling, controls and semiconductor-adjacent components.

Who may benefit / hurt: U.S., Mexican and Canadian component makers with documented regional content may gain. Assemblers dependent on non-North-American subassemblies may face redesign and compliance costs.

Action: Build BOM-level origin maps now and identify the highest-value inputs that can be dual-sourced or localized in North America.
Risk check: Negotiating concepts are not final legal text; do not assume a specific regional-content percentage.
Wall Street Journal · Sep. 16
7 · Texas–Mexico · Natural Gas

West Texas Express tests demand for a new Waha-to-border gas corridor

What happened: Enbridge’s non-binding open season for West Texas Express runs through September 25. The proposed system would extend more than 150 miles from Waha toward El Paso, Hudspeth County and U.S.–Mexico border delivery points, with initial capacity up to 2 Bcf/d.

Why it matters: Additional Permian takeaway could support power generation, industrial growth and Mexico gas demand while easing Waha constraints.

Who may benefit / hurt: Producers, pipeline contractors, compression, metering, electrical and civil firms may benefit. Competing takeaway systems face commercial pressure.

Action: Map likely border delivery nodes and contractor scopes before the September 25 open-season close.
Risk check: The open season is non-binding. Commercial support, routing, permits and final investment approval remain outstanding.

8 · Foreign-Trade Zones · Semiconductors

Texas’ semiconductor FTZ docket creates immediate customs work

What happened: Ichor Systems’ Austin semiconductor-equipment production matter has an October 5 comment deadline, and Dongjin Semichem Texas’ Killeen production matter is open through October 20.

Why it matters: Semiconductor equipment and specialty chemicals often contain high-value imported inputs where duty deferral, re-export treatment and inventory timing can change working capital.

9 · Opportunity Zones · JETI

Texas’ 605 OZ 2.0 nominations create a new decade-long incentive layer

What happened: Texas nominated 605 census tracts in 105 counties for the 2027–2036 Opportunity Zone cycle. Under JETI, qualifying projects in qualified Opportunity Zones can receive an additional 25% limitation on taxable value.

Why it matters: Federal capital-gains treatment and Texas school-tax economics can materially alter after-tax industrial returns when the underlying site already works.

Who may benefit / hurt: Capital-intensive factories and infrastructure on strong sites can gain. Speculative buyers who chase incentive labels without utility and labor diligence can overpay.

Action: Overlay OZ nominations with JETI, FTZ, power, water, labor, rail/highways, flood risk and local abatements.
Risk check: Treasury certification and JETI approval are separate processes.

10 · Texas · Manufacturing Incentives

Fort Worth approves incentives for a proposed $433.8M Carrier plant

What happened: Fort Worth approved a tax-abatement package supporting Carrier Global’s proposed advanced-manufacturing facility in the Alliance area. Public reporting tied to city records describes $433.8 million of investment, 495 jobs averaging at least $75,000 and a Texas Enterprise Zone nomination; Carrier is expected to choose a site by September 30.

Why it matters: The project would link advanced HVAC and data-center cooling manufacturing to North Texas’ rapidly expanding AI infrastructure cluster.

Who may benefit / hurt: Sheet metal, controls, electrical, refrigerant systems, automation, testing, construction and logistics suppliers could gain. Competing states and Texas firms facing labor competition may be pressured.

Action: Prepare vendor and workforce qualification packages now rather than waiting for final site selection.
Risk check: The city incentive is approved, but Carrier’s Fort Worth site selection is not final and performance-based incentives include job/investment conditions.
