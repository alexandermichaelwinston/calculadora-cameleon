1 · U.S.–Mexico · Trade Deal

Mexico and Washington accelerate a bilateral trade deal before the U.S. midterms

What happened: Reuters reports U.S. and Mexican officials are sprinting toward a bilateral agreement before the November 3 U.S. elections after U.S.–Canada talks broke down. Mexico wants relief from Section 232 tariffs on autos, steel and aluminum; Washington is pressing for more U.S. content in vehicles and tighter scrutiny of Chinese investment.

Why it matters: A bilateral package could reshape the economics of autos, steel, aluminum, electronics and supplier localization before the broader USMCA review is settled.

Who may benefit / hurt: Suppliers with traceable U.S./Mexico content could gain; assemblers dependent on Chinese or other third-country inputs may face higher localization costs.

Action: Build a bill-of-materials origin map and quote 2027 business under current rules, tariff relief and higher-U.S.-content scenarios.
Risk check: This is an active negotiation, not final law. Do not change long-term sourcing until the adopted tariff and origin rules are published.
Reuters · Sep. 11
2 · Energy · Freight

Oil eases from the morning spike, but U.S. diesel hits a record above $6 per gallon

What happened: Brent was around $104.35 and WTI about $99.07 late Friday morning after earlier highs, while both remained on track for weekly gains above 8%. Reuters reports U.S. diesel prices reached a record above $6 per gallon as Middle East shipping disruptions tightened refined-product supply.

Why it matters: Diesel is the direct cost line for trucking, agriculture, construction, mining and many industrial supply chains; sustained fuel inflation can erase margins even when crude later falls.

Who may benefit / hurt: Producers, refiners and oilfield services can gain; fleets, distributors, contractors and manufacturers face higher landed costs.

Action: Reset fuel surcharges, freight allowances and bid-escalation clauses, and separate diesel assumptions from crude-oil assumptions.
Risk check: A temporary shipping deal through Hormuz could quickly reduce the geopolitical premium; keep a normalization case in every forecast.
Reuters · Sep. 11
3 · FX · Mexico

Latest Banxico FIX determination is 16.9722 MXN/USD; today’s liability-payment rate is 16.8947

What happened: Banco de México lists the September 10 FIX determination at 16.9722 pesos per dollar. For obligations payable in Mexico on September 11, Banxico shows 16.8947, reflecting the prior published reference.

Why it matters: The peso remains strong versus late 2025, raising the dollar cost of Mexican wages and local services while making imported equipment cheaper for Mexico-based factories.

Who may benefit / hurt: Mexican equipment importers may benefit; exporters earning dollars while paying peso expenses can see margin compression.

Action: Use FX bands, short quote-validity periods, natural hedges and contract adjustment clauses instead of assuming a fixed peso rate.
Risk check: FIX determination, DOF publication, payment rate, interbank close and retail-bank rates are different series. Match the rate to the contract purpose.
Banco de México · Sep. 11
4 · Mexico · Industrial Real Estate

Mexico’s FIBRAs are becoming a financing channel for manufacturing, logistics and AI infrastructure

What happened: BIVA CEO Maria Ariza says Mexico’s listed FIBRAs can finance industrial parks, logistics assets and infrastructure serving North American supply chains. Reuters notes Mexico exported roughly $50 billion in computer and electronic equipment in Q1 2026, nearly double a year earlier.

Why it matters: Nearshoring value is expanding from labor arbitrage into power-ready buildings, warehousing, connectivity and specialized industrial infrastructure.

Who may benefit / hurt: Utilities-ready industrial landlords and logistics developers may gain; sites with weak power, water, security or legal certainty can underperform.

Action: Underwrite Mexican industrial property on firm power, water, security, highway/rail, customs access and tenant depth before relying on incentive headlines.
Risk check: FIBRA enthusiasm does not guarantee occupancy or returns; legal certainty and energy availability remain material risks.
Reuters · Sep. 9
5 · Mexico · Nearshoring Reality Check

Record FDI still masks a weak new-factory pipeline

What happened: Mexico attracted nearly $35 billion of FDI in the first half of 2026, but only 7.8% represented new investment; greenfield investment fell nearly by half in 2025. Reuters says USMCA uncertainty is causing some companies to delay or diversify projects.

Why it matters: Existing plants and established industrial clusters are receiving reinvestment, while speculative greenfield sites carry greater timing risk.

Who may benefit / hurt: Serviced parks with labor and utilities may outperform raw-land nearshoring bets; developers dependent on rapid new-factory commitments face more risk.

Action: Stage capex, use expandable leases and compare delivered cost against Texas, the Dominican Republic and other nearshore alternatives.
Risk check: Headline FDI includes reinvested earnings. Do not equate total FDI with new construction spending.
Reuters · Sep. 1
6 · U.S.–Mexico · Trade Volume

July U.S. imports from Mexico hit a record $60.5 billion

What happened: Census/BEA reported July imports from Mexico at a record $60.5 billion. U.S. capital-goods imports also reached a record $140.3 billion, reflecting strong machinery, computer and semiconductor demand.

Why it matters: The volume confirms how deeply Mexican manufacturing is embedded in U.S. demand even as policy pushes more North American and U.S. content.

Who may benefit / hurt: Border logistics, customs, warehousing and regional component suppliers benefit from throughput; politically sensitive import categories remain exposed to trade action.

Action: Identify high-volume Mexican import categories where a Texas or regional substitute can win on lead time, compliance, resilience or service.
Risk check: Monthly trade data are volatile and revised; use several months of data before making facility decisions.
U.S. Census / BEA · Sep. 3
7 · Nuevo Laredo · Freight Infrastructure

MEX II corridor completion removes a long-standing approach-road bottleneck at the busiest commercial border

What happened: Nuevo Laredo completed the long-delayed MEX II corridor and vehicle bridge with about 283.2 million pesos of municipal investment. The route connects industrial areas toward the World Trade Bridge, serving a corridor with more than 16,000 commercial trucks daily.

Why it matters: Bridge throughput depends on feeder roads, yard access and drayage time as much as inspection booths.

Who may benefit / hurt: Laredo/Nuevo Laredo carriers, yards, customs brokers and industrial parks can benefit from better turns; older congested routes may lose relative value.

Action: Re-time door-to-bridge drayage and refresh warehouse/site comparisons using actual post-opening truck-turn data.
Risk check: Road capacity does not solve customs IT outages, inspection delays or U.S.-side bottlenecks.
Laredo Morning Times · Sep. 9
8 · Colombia–Anáhuac · Border Development

Two new freight bridges and a Plan México industrial hub could create a second major Laredo-area growth node

What happened: Nuevo León plans two new freight bridges at Colombia with construction targeted for May 2027 and completion by 2029. The roughly $200 million bridge program is paired with the Anáhuac-Colombia Industrial Hub and tax/administrative incentives under Plan México.

Why it matters: A higher-capacity alternative corridor can reshape trucking, warehouse and industrial-land economics between Texas and Nuevo León.

Who may benefit / hurt: Logistics firms, civil contractors, truck yards, brokers and manufacturers seeking redundancy may benefit; speculative land buyers can be hurt if utilities or traffic arrive slowly.

Action: Map land, power, water, highway access and drayage economics before the construction cycle accelerates.
Risk check: Construction timing, bridge approvals and company-specific incentive eligibility must be verified before underwriting.
Laredo Morning Times · Sep. 9Plan México · Official
9 · Texas · FTZ + Opportunity Zones

Texas has immediate FTZ deadlines and a new OZ 2.0/JETI incentive stack to model

What happened: Current FTZ matters include CGT in New Braunfels with comments due September 14, Abbott in Irving and Ascentec’s Texas case due September 15, and Ichor Systems in Austin due October 5. Texas has also nominated 605 tracts in 105 counties for OZ 2.0; JETI rules allow a larger 75% taxable-value discount when an eligible project is entirely inside a qualifying Opportunity Zone.

Why it matters: Customs and property-tax incentives can materially change site economics when combined with strong logistics and utilities.

Who may benefit / hurt: Import-intensive manufacturers and capital-heavy projects can benefit; projects chosen for incentives instead of operating fundamentals can underperform.

Action: Build one site matrix combining FTZ, OZ, JETI, local abatements, power, water, workforce and logistics before negotiating land.
Risk check: Each program has separate qualification, approval and compliance rules. State OZ nomination is not Treasury certification.

10 · U.S. Power · Data Centers

Data-center power is moving from a growth story to a permitting, grid and ratepayer discipline story

What happened: The U.S. House is expected to vote next week on a bipartisan bill aimed at limiting data-center-driven electricity costs for households. In Texas, ERCOT’s Batch Zero process now applies to large loads of 75 MW or more and is forcing projects to prove interconnection readiness.

Why it matters: Power availability, queue position, financial security and local rate impacts are becoming core site-selection criteria for AI and advanced-manufacturing campuses.

Who may benefit / hurt: Sites with real substations, generation, transmission and permits gain scarcity value; speculative powered-land projects face higher scrutiny.

Action: Treat verified megawatts, interconnection classification, transformer lead times and water as due-diligence items before valuing data-center land.
Risk check: A queue request is not an energization commitment. Federal legislation and ERCOT classifications can still change.
