1 · Mexico · Inflation

Headline inflation rises to 3.42%, while core inflation keeps easing

What happened: Mexico's annual inflation accelerated to 3.42% in the first half of September from 3.26% a month earlier, above the 3.37% Reuters-poll forecast. Core inflation eased to 3.79% from 3.93%.

Why it matters: The mix matters for financing, wages and pricing: headline pressures picked up, but underlying inflation remains inside Banco de México's 3% ±1-point target band.

Who may benefit / hurt: Businesses with pricing power and peso cash yields may benefit; fixed-price contractors, consumers and rate-sensitive borrowers can be pressured if inflation stays sticky.

Action: Refresh Mexico wage, rent, freight and financing assumptions separately instead of applying one inflation number across every cost line.
Risk check: Half-month inflation is a short-period reading. Banxico policy depends on the broader inflation path, expectations, growth and currency conditions.
Reuters via Investing · Sep. 24
2 · Mexico · Growth

Mexico's July economy grows 0.8% month over month — four times the consensus forecast

What happened: INEGI data show economic activity expanded 0.8% in July versus a Reuters-poll forecast of 0.2%. Services, including transport and wholesale trade, rose 1.2%; construction/manufacturing and other secondary activity rose 0.5%. Year over year, activity increased 3.4%.

Why it matters: Stronger transport, wholesale and industrial activity supports the demand case for logistics space, freight, working capital and supplier expansion.

Who may benefit / hurt: Carriers, distributors, industrial landlords and manufacturers with Mexico exposure may benefit; capacity-constrained operators can face higher labor and service costs.

Action: Recheck northern-Mexico warehouse absorption, trucking demand and customer forecasts before assuming the softer June trend continued.
Risk check: One strong month does not establish a new trend; tariffs, FX and USMCA negotiations remain material risks.
Reuters via Investing · Sep. 24
3 · Mexico · Dollar/Peso

Banxico FIX moves to 17.5030 MXN/USD — roughly 1.16% weaker peso than the prior FIX

What happened: Banco de México set the September 23 official FIX at 17.5030 pesos per dollar, up from 17.3015 for September 22.

Why it matters: A weaker peso can reduce dollar-equivalent Mexican labor and local-service costs while raising the peso cost of dollar equipment, imported inputs, fuel and debt.

Who may benefit / hurt: Dollar-paid exporters with peso expenses can benefit; Mexican importers and dollar borrowers may be hurt.

Action: Use FX bands, short quote validity, natural hedges and automatic currency-adjustment clauses for cross-border work.
Risk check: FIX is an official reference based on wholesale quotations; it is not a retail-bank or executable spot rate.

4 · Mexico · Korea Nearshoring

Lee-Sheinbaum summit puts AI, supply chains and Mexico's industrial platform in focus

What happened: South Korean President Lee Jae Myung is in Mexico for today's summit with President Claudia Sheinbaum. Lee said Mexico is a strategic platform into North America and highlighted AI, supply chains, semiconductors, aerospace, EVs and energy as areas where Korean technology can combine with Mexico's industrial base.

Why it matters: Korean manufacturers already operate substantial North American supply chains. Deeper cooperation can translate into supplier localization, industrial-park demand, power needs and cross-border freight.

Who may benefit / hurt: Mexican industrial developers, utilities, logistics providers and qualified North American suppliers may gain; incumbent vendors that cannot meet regional-content, quality or cost requirements may lose share.

Action: Build Korea-focused site and supplier packages showing firm power, water, customs access, labor, incentives and verified North American content.
Risk check: Summit statements and MOUs are not factory awards. Wait for named companies, sites, capex and binding agreements before underwriting projects.
Yonhap · Sep. 24
5 · Border · Livestock Trade

Santa Teresa reopens today, restoring the biggest Mexican-cattle inspection route

What happened: The Santa Teresa, New Mexico livestock port is reopening to Mexican cattle under enhanced screwworm safeguards. Initial flows are expected around 750 to 1,600 cattle per day, below the roughly 3,000-per-day peak capacity.

Why it matters: The port historically handles a large share of Mexican cattle imports and is important to Southwest and Texas feedlot supply. Reopening improves trade flow after the long animal-health closure.

Who may benefit / hurt: Chihuahua ranchers, haulers, veterinarians, feedlots and meat processors may benefit; operators face added inspection, treatment and traceability costs.

Action: Confirm animal ID, inspection, treatment, trailer sanitation and appointment requirements before dispatch; build capacity gradually around observed crossing times.
Risk check: New World screwworm is contained, not eradicated. USDA can alter or pause flows if disease risk rises.
Reuters-syndicated · Sep. 24
6 · USMCA · U.S. Sourcing

Mexico's USMCA strategy explicitly points toward buying more U.S. goods

What happened: President Sheinbaum said Mexico intends to increase U.S. imports while reducing purchases from other countries as the USMCA review proceeds and Washington presses Mexico over the U.S. trade deficit.

Why it matters: Machinery, chemicals, components, agricultural inputs and other procurement could shift toward suppliers able to document reliable U.S. or North American origin.

Who may benefit / hurt: U.S. exporters, Mexican distributors and North American manufacturers may benefit; non-regional suppliers into Mexico face localization pressure.

Action: Build a replace-the-import table for your category: current origin, U.S./North American alternative, delivered cost, lead time, duty exposure and rules-of-origin status.
Risk check: This is a policy signal, not a product-level purchase mandate or revised customs rule.
Reuters · Sep. 21
7 · Mexico · Border Rail

Saltillo-Nuevo Laredo rail build reaches 9.86%; 5G rail-telecom award is due September 25

What happened: Mexico's project portal reports the 396-km Saltillo-Nuevo Laredo railway at 9.86% physical progress as of September 23, with estimated investment of MXN 175.7 billion. A separate international tender covers 5G standalone railway communications across the Saltillo-Nuevo Laredo and Mexico-Querétaro-Irapuato corridors, with the award scheduled for September 25.

Why it matters: The corridor links Monterrey's industrial region to Nuevo Laredo, reinforcing long-term demand for stations, civil works, telecom, power, signaling and border-adjacent development.

Who may benefit / hurt: Rail contractors, telecom integrators, civil firms, materials suppliers and industrial landholders along the corridor may gain; schedules and procurement can shift as work packages evolve.

Action: Track individual tenders rather than the headline project alone; identify signaling, telecom, electrical, station, earthwork and maintenance packages suitable for your capabilities.
Risk check: Project-portal data are preliminary and individual bid documents control scope, qualification and timing.

8 · Energy · Diesel

Diesel remains the sharper cost risk even as crude whipsaws

What happened: U.S. diesel inventories were 107.9 million barrels on September 11, the lowest seasonal level in records dating to 1982, and EIA expects distillate stocks to remain unusually low through much of 2027. On September 24, Brent was again above $105 intraday as U.S.-Iran diplomacy showed little progress.

Why it matters: Diesel directly drives trucking, agriculture, heavy construction and manufacturing logistics, so freight costs can stay high even when crude periodically falls.

Who may benefit / hurt: Refiners and fuel-distribution businesses may benefit from strong margins; fleets, builders, farmers and manufacturers remain pressured.

Action: Index fuel surcharges to diesel, consolidate routes, reduce empty miles and shorten validity periods on fuel-heavy bids.
Risk check: A reported 90-day U.S. diesel-export ban has not been enacted; the White House denied that specific plan. Policy and geopolitical headlines can move prices sharply.

9 · Texas · Foreign-Trade Zones

Austin and Killeen semiconductor FTZ cases create immediate customs-planning windows

What happened: The U.S. FTZ Board lists Ichor Systems' Austin production case with comments due October 5 and Dongjin Semichem Texas' Killeen production case with comments due October 20.

Why it matters: Semiconductor equipment and specialty chemicals can carry meaningful imported-content duties and working-capital costs. FTZ treatment can improve economics in the right fact pattern.

Who may benefit / hurt: Import-intensive manufacturers, customs specialists and bonded-logistics operators may benefit; companies with weak HTS/origin controls can incur penalties or overestimate savings.

Action: Model each major SKU by HTS code, origin, duty, inventory dwell, re-export share and special-tariff exposure before deciding whether FTZ production authority adds value.
Risk check: Section 232/301 treatment and foreign-status rules can materially change savings; Federal Register notices control.

10 · Texas · Opportunity Zones + JETI

Texas OZ 2.0 nominations can potentially stack with an enhanced JETI limitation

What happened: Texas nominated 605 census tracts across 105 counties for the 2027-2036 Opportunity Zone cycle. Texas says qualifying JETI projects located in qualified Opportunity Zones are eligible for an additional 25% limitation on taxable value.

Why it matters: Federal capital-gains treatment plus Texas school-tax economics can materially alter after-tax industrial returns when the underlying site already works operationally.

Who may benefit / hurt: Qualifying capital-intensive manufacturing, high-tech infrastructure and dispatchable generation can benefit; speculative land buyers can overpay for an incentive label without utilities or workforce.

Action: Overlay OZ nominations with JETI thresholds, FTZs, power, water, flood risk, labor, rail/highway and local abatements in one site-selection model.
Risk check: Treasury certification and JETI approval are separate processes, and eligibility depends on project type, investment and job thresholds.
