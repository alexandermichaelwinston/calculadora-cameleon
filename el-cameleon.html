1 · Texas / Korea · Power + AI

South Korea formally launches a $22.3B Encinal power project aimed at Texas AI data centers

What happened: South Korea officially unveiled implementation plans for three major U.S. energy initiatives under its $350 billion strategic investment package. The lead project is a $22.3 billion gas-fired power development in Encinal, Texas, intended to directly power AI data centers, with initial commercial operations targeted for 2029.

Why it matters: The project links trade policy, foreign direct investment, power generation and AI infrastructure in one transaction. If it advances, it can create years of demand for gas supply, turbines, substations, transmission, water systems, controls and heavy construction.

Who may benefit / hurt: Texas EPC, pipeline, electrical, fabrication, cooling-water and industrial-service firms may gain. Competing large-load projects may face tighter labor, equipment, gas and interconnection capacity.

Action: Map likely turbine, gas, high-voltage, water, cooling, civil, controls and commissioning packages now and identify South Korean OEM/EPC partners before formal procurement accelerates.
Risk check: The project has been announced, but schedule, permitting, contracting structure and final investment mechanics can still change before construction and commercial operation.
Reuters · Oct. 1
2 · Trade Policy · Tariffs

U.S. trade policy is hardening around tariffs, excess capacity and strategic supply chains

What happened: At the G20 trade ministers meeting, U.S. Trade Representative Jamieson Greer urged major economies to confront industrial overcapacity and reconsider the traditional most-favored-nation tariff framework. Separately, 28 market-oriented economies agreed on a steel framework encouraging more anti-dumping, anti-subsidy and safeguard actions against excess-capacity sources.

Why it matters: The direction of travel is toward more origin scrutiny and more product-specific trade barriers, not less. That raises the value of auditable North American content as U.S.–Mexico negotiations continue.

Who may benefit / hurt: Domestic and regional steelmakers, processors and traceable North American suppliers may benefit. Importers dependent on low-cost steel or steel-containing goods from overcapacity markets may face higher duties and documentation burdens.

Action: Add country-of-melt-and-pour, tariff exposure and alternate-source fields to procurement records for steel-intensive products and model U.S./Mexico substitutes before new duties appear.
Risk check: The G20 and OECD framework does not itself impose a new U.S. tariff. Actual cost changes require country-specific investigations, rules or customs actions.
Reuters · Oct. 1Reuters · Sep. 30
3 · Canada · Manufacturing

Canadian factory growth slows to a six-month low as North American trade frictions bite

What happened: Canada's September manufacturing PMI fell to 51.5 from 53.0 in August, the slowest expansion since March. New orders slipped below 50, input-cost inflation rose sharply and business confidence fell to a nine-month low after new U.S. restrictions on selected Canadian alcohol, motorcycles and dairy products took effect September 29.

Why it matters: It is fresh evidence that North American trade friction is affecting real factory activity, supplier confidence and delivery performance even inside the USMCA region.

Who may benefit / hurt: U.S. and Mexican substitute suppliers may gain opportunities where Canadian sourcing becomes more expensive or uncertain. Canadian manufacturers and U.S. buyers reliant on exposed inputs face higher costs and weaker demand visibility.

Action: Review Canadian-origin inputs by HTS classification, lead time and tariff risk, then prequalify at least one U.S. or Mexican alternate source for exposed SKUs.
Risk check: Canada's manufacturing sector is still expanding overall, and the restrictions are product-specific rather than a blanket tariff on all Canadian goods.
Reuters · Oct. 1
4 · USMCA · U.S.–Mexico

The next formal U.S.–Mexico trade round has moved into October while technical talks continue

What happened: Mexico says the fourth formal negotiating round on changes to the North American trade framework has been pushed into October, while technical coordination with USTR continues. Key issues include automotive content, Chinese investment, digital rules and Mexican requests for relief from U.S. steel, aluminum and automotive tariffs.

Why it matters: Manufacturers are making 2027 sourcing and investment decisions before the trade rules are settled. The broader U.S. tariff stance announced at the G20 reinforces the likelihood of tougher origin and anti-circumvention enforcement.

Who may benefit / hurt: Companies with verifiable U.S./Mexico content and flexible sourcing are better positioned. Auto, metals and import-heavy manufacturers remain exposed to sudden landed-cost changes.

Action: Maintain three commercial models: current rules, partial tariff relief and tighter North American origin requirements; avoid long fixed-price commitments that assume one outcome.
Risk check: Negotiating statements do not change customs treatment. Price changes should follow published legal text, tariff schedules or customs guidance.
Reuters · Sep. 24Reuters · Oct. 1
5 · Mexico · Dollar/Peso

Banxico's latest official FIX is 18.0692 MXN/USD

What happened: Banco de México set the September 30 FIX at 18.0692 pesos per U.S. dollar, essentially flat versus the prior day's 18.0710 after the peso weakened sharply during the second half of September.

Why it matters: FX directly changes the economics of cross-border labor, leases, imports, fuel, dollar-denominated equipment and debt.

Who may benefit / hurt: Dollar-earning exporters with peso expenses can benefit from a weaker peso. Mexican importers and companies servicing dollar liabilities face higher peso costs.

Action: Use FX bands and automatic currency-adjustment clauses and stress-test projects at 18, 19 and 20 MXN/USD instead of relying on one exchange-rate assumption.
Risk check: FIX is an official reference based on wholesale market quotations, not an executable retail or bank transaction rate.
Banco de México · Sep. 30 FIX
6 · Semiconductors · Texas

TSMC is evaluating a potential Texas chip-manufacturing investment

What happened: Reuters reports that TSMC, the world's largest contract chipmaker, is evaluating a potential Texas investment in addition to the $265 billion it has already committed to Arizona. TSMC says no Texas plan has been finalized.

Why it matters: Even an active evaluation raises the strategic value of Texas semiconductor sites, power, water, cleanroom construction, process-gas infrastructure, suppliers and workforce programs.

Who may benefit / hurt: Texas utilities, semiconductor equipment suppliers, industrial developers, cleanroom contractors and specialty-chemical/logistics firms could gain if a project is selected. Competing U.S. locations would face another major Texas bid.

Action: Prepare semiconductor-ready site packages combining firm power, ultrapure-water capacity, wastewater, workforce, FTZ, JETI, TSIF and supplier proximity before any formal site process becomes public.
Risk check: There is no finalized Texas site, capex figure, construction schedule or job commitment. Do not speculate on land or vendor awards based only on the evaluation report.
Reuters · Sep. 30 / updated Oct. 1
7 · Nuevo León / Laredo · Nearshoring

Nuevo León advances two new Laredo crossings while pitching more nearshoring investment

What happened: Nuevo León's governor is on a September 30–October 2 U.S. investment trip. The agenda includes follow-up on two additional Laredo border crossings, infrastructure, energy and water projects, and nearshoring finance discussions. The state also announced at least $400 million of additional LEGO manufacturing investment for a new production building and warehouse expansion.

Why it matters: More bridge capacity and new manufacturing investment reinforce the Monterrey–Nuevo León–Laredo corridor as a core North American production and logistics spine.

Who may benefit / hurt: Customs brokers, carriers, warehouses, civil contractors, utility providers and industrial developers on both sides of the border may benefit. Competing crossings and sites may lose relative traffic or investment share.

Action: Map developable land, truck yards, warehouse capacity, utility extensions and customs technology around Colombia-Laredo and pair those assets with Monterrey/Nuevo León supplier demand.
Risk check: Bridge concepts still require permits, financing, design and binational approvals. Announced factory investment does not equal immediate subcontract awards.
Nuevo León Government · Sep. 24
8 · Energy / Logistics · Gulf Coast

Texas-region oil output rises while refiners capture record fuel margins

What happened: The Dallas Fed says oil and gas output across Texas, Louisiana and New Mexico rose in Q3. U.S. oil production hit a weekly record of 13.955 million barrels per day in the week to September 25, while diesel and gasoline crack spreads hit record highs and refinery utilization averaged 96.3% in Q3.

Why it matters: The Americas are simultaneously seeing strong upstream output and expensive refined fuels. That matters for trucking, construction, agriculture and Mexico/Latin America fuel flows.

Who may benefit / hurt: Producers, refiners, oilfield services and Gulf Coast terminals can benefit. Truck fleets, contractors and manufacturers remain exposed to high diesel costs and possible export-policy intervention.

Action: Separate crude and diesel assumptions in bids, use diesel-specific escalation clauses, and model export-policy scenarios for Gulf Coast supply into Mexico and Latin America.
Risk check: Commodity prices and refinery margins are highly volatile; reported U.S. diesel-export restrictions remain a policy possibility rather than a confirmed blanket ban.
Reuters · Sep. 30
9 · Texas · Foreign-Trade Zones

Four Texas FTZ cases have live October and November comment deadlines

What happened: The U.S. Foreign-Trade Zones Board lists Ichor Systems in Austin with comments due October 5, Dongjin Semichem Texas in Killeen due October 20, and Enerflex Energy Systems in Houston plus Perimeter Global Logistics in Grand Prairie due November 3.

Why it matters: FTZ treatment can affect duty timing, inventory carrying cost and re-export economics across semiconductor equipment, specialty chemicals, energy systems and logistics.

Who may benefit / hurt: Import-intensive manufacturers, customs specialists and bonded-logistics providers may benefit. Companies with weak HTS, origin or special-tariff controls can create compliance risk or overstate savings.

Action: Run SKU-level models covering HTS classification, country of origin, standard duty, Section 232/301 exposure, dwell time and re-export share before changing facility or sourcing strategy.
Risk check: Federal Register notices control each case, and FTZ status does not automatically eliminate special tariffs.

10 · Texas · Incentives

OZ 2.0, JETI, DEAAG and Enterprise Zone deadlines form a Q4 incentive stack

What happened: Texas has nominated 608 census tracts in 106 counties for Opportunity Zone 2.0, with federal certification expected by November 28. JETI offers qualifying projects a 50% school-district M&O taxable-value limitation and an additional 25% limitation for qualifying projects in Opportunity Zones. FY27 DEAAG applications close November 6, and the next Enterprise Zone deadline is December 1.

Why it matters: Federal capital-gains treatment, school-tax limitations, infrastructure grants and sales/use-tax refunds can materially change site economics when layered on top of viable utilities, workforce and logistics.

Who may benefit / hurt: Qualifying manufacturers, dispatchable generation, high-tech infrastructure, critical-infrastructure projects and defense communities may benefit. Speculative projects on weak sites can still fail despite incentives.

Action: Use one site-selection model combining power, water, gas, flood risk, workforce, FTZ, OZ, JETI, DEAAG, Enterprise Zone and local abatements before committing capital.
Risk check: Each program has separate eligibility, timing and approvals; incentives should enhance a viable project rather than rescue a weak site.
