1 · U.S.–Mexico · Trade

Trump–Sheinbaum call produces “some agreements” as a bilateral deal moves closer

What happened: President Claudia Sheinbaum said Friday that she and U.S. President Donald Trump reached some agreements during a Wednesday call, while Washington accelerates efforts to reach a bilateral trade deal with Mexico before the U.S. midterm elections. She did not disclose the terms.

Why it matters: A bilateral package could reset tariff treatment, automotive sourcing, metals costs and investment planning before the broader USMCA process is fully resolved.

Who may benefit / hurt: Companies with auditable North American content, flexible sourcing and U.S.–Mexico capacity may gain. Import-heavy assemblers and suppliers dependent on unresolved tariff treatment remain exposed.

Action: Maintain three 2027 pricing models—current rules, partial tariff relief, and stricter regional-content requirements—and update them as legal text appears.
Risk check: No details of the agreements were released. A presidential call is not a tariff schedule, customs rule or finalized trade agreement.

2 · USMCA · AI Hardware

AI servers and chips remain a rules-of-origin localization opportunity

What happened: U.S. negotiators are pressing Mexico for tighter North American origin and traceability standards on AI hardware, including servers and chips, as part of the USMCA review. Proposed concepts reportedly include auto-style regional-content rules.

Why it matters: Mexico’s fast-growing electronics and server assembly base could require deeper U.S.–Mexico–Canada sourcing of racks, power systems, cooling, electronics and components.

Who may benefit / hurt: Regional component manufacturers and traceability/compliance providers can benefit; assemblers relying heavily on China-origin subassemblies may face redesign or higher compliance costs.

Action: Build BOM-level origin maps now and identify the highest-value non-North-American components that can be dual-sourced or localized.
Risk check: Negotiating concepts are not final legal text; do not assume a 75% threshold or final covered-product list.
WSJ · Sep. 16
3 · Dollar · Peso

Banxico’s latest official FIX is 17.1872 MXN/USD

What happened: Banco de México set the September 17 FIX at 17.1872 pesos per dollar. Friday morning interbank quotations were around the low 17.1s.

Why it matters: A weaker peso can lower the dollar-equivalent cost of Mexican wages, rent and local services while increasing the peso cost of dollar-priced machinery, fuel and imported inputs.

Who may benefit / hurt: Dollar-paid exporters with peso costs may benefit; Mexican importers and borrowers with dollar liabilities can be hurt.

Action: Use explicit FX bands, shorter quote-validity periods, natural hedges and contract currency-adjustment clauses.
Risk check: FIX is an official reference rate published after noon on Mexican banking days; it is not the same as a live spot or retail-bank execution price.

4 · Oil · Logistics

Oil stays above $100 as Saudi Arabia reroutes supply around damaged infrastructure

What happened: Brent traded around $104.49 and WTI around $102.79 Friday as markets weighed damaged Saudi East–West pipeline pumping stations against Aramco efforts to move roughly 60 million barrels through Gulf/Oman ship-to-ship arrangements during September and October.

Why it matters: Diesel, trucking, chemicals, plastics, asphalt and construction remain exposed to a geopolitical energy premium even while alternate supply routes reduce the worst-case shortage risk.

Who may benefit / hurt: Producers, refiners and fuel-logistics providers can benefit; fleets, manufacturers and fixed-price contractors face higher operating costs.

Action: Keep fuel-escalation clauses active and model both sustained $100+ crude and a rapid normalization case.
Risk check: Repair timelines and tanker flows remain uncertain; energy prices can move sharply in either direction.

5 · Texas · Solar Manufacturing

Tesla’s proposed $10.1B Fort Bend solar plant advances through JETI

What happened: Lamar CISD approved the JETI tax-limitation agreement for Tesla’s proposed Project Crystal Sun. The application describes about $10.1 billion of investment, 9,712 full-time jobs by 2033 and roughly $1.3 billion in annual payroll if Fort Bend is selected.

Why it matters: The project could create a major Houston-area solar-cell/module manufacturing cluster and demand for electrical, automation, process-utility, construction, logistics and workforce services.

Who may benefit / hurt: Texas suppliers and contractors could gain; local infrastructure, roads, utilities and taxpayers carry execution risk if the project or hiring ramp changes.

Action: Prepare vendor qualification packages now for electrical distribution, clean manufacturing, water treatment, automation, material handling and industrial construction.
Risk check: Tesla has not made its final site decision and another non-Texas location remains under consideration; state approval is still required.
Houston Chronicle · Sep. 17
6 · Texas · Power + AI

A $22.3B, 6.3-GW Encinal power project is under discussion in the Korea-U.S. investment package

What happened: Reuters reports that a combined-cycle gas plant in Encinal, Texas is among projects under discussion for South Korea’s $350 billion U.S. investment package. Media reports describe roughly $22.3 billion of investment and 6.3 GW of capacity, staged first with 1.4 GW of gas turbines and later 4.9 GW of combined cycle.

Why it matters: A project of that scale would create major demand for turbines, EPC, substations, transmission, gas pipelines, switchgear, cooling, water and construction while supporting AI data centers and semiconductor plants.

Who may benefit / hurt: Texas energy, engineering and construction suppliers could gain; competing power projects and infrastructure-constrained communities could face pressure.

Action: Build supplier and contractor capability packages for power generation, transmission, gas, water and controls—but treat the project as a watchlist, not booked backlog.
Risk check: South Korea’s Industry Ministry says details are not set. The project is under discussion, not a final investment decision.
Reuters · Sep. 18
7 · Mexico · Fuel Trade

Pemex refinery weakness sharply increased Mexico’s fuel-import dependence

What happened: Reuters reports Pemex Q2 diesel output fell 11% and gasoline output 13% from the prior quarter, while diesel imports jumped 134% and gasoline imports 45%. Import values rose to about $1.51 billion for diesel and $4.55 billion for gasoline.

Why it matters: Mexico’s refinery reliability directly affects trucking costs, industrial fuel availability, public finances and refined-product flows from the U.S. Gulf Coast.

Who may benefit / hurt: Gulf Coast refiners, terminals, storage and fuel logistics may benefit if import demand stays elevated; Pemex finances and Mexican fuel-intensive businesses face pressure.

Action: Monitor Gulf Coast terminal utilization, marine loading, border distribution and commercial fuel-contract opportunities tied to Mexico.
Risk check: Recent import growth is not guaranteed to persist; refinery maintenance, utilization, policy and global fuel prices can change quickly.
Reuters · Sep. 17
8 · Foreign-Trade Zones · Texas

Texas’ live FTZ semiconductor docket runs through Austin and Killeen

What happened: The U.S. Foreign-Trade Zones Board lists Ichor Systems’ Austin production application with comments due October 5 and Dongjin Semichem Texas’ Killeen production application with comments due October 20.

Why it matters: Texas’ semiconductor ecosystem is broadening from fabs into equipment, precision manufacturing and specialty chemicals where customs treatment can influence landed cost and working capital.

Who may benefit / hurt: Import-intensive manufacturers, customs specialists and bonded-logistics operators can benefit when savings exceed compliance costs; weak classification controls create penalty and duty risk.

Action: Run SKU-by-SKU FTZ models using HTS code, country of origin, duty rate, inventory dwell and re-export percentage.
Risk check: Section 232/301 treatment, product-specific authorization and privileged-foreign-status rules can reduce or eliminate expected savings.

9 · Opportunity Zones · JETI

Texas’ 605 OZ 2.0 nominations can stack with an enhanced JETI limitation

What happened: Texas nominated 605 census tracts in 105 counties for the 2027–2036 Opportunity Zone cycle. JETI provides qualifying projects a 50% school-district M&O appraised-value limitation, with an additional 25% limitation for projects located in qualified Opportunity Zones.

Why it matters: Federal capital-gains treatment and Texas school-tax economics can materially change the after-tax return of large manufacturing and infrastructure projects.

Who may benefit / hurt: Capital-intensive projects on fundamentally strong sites can benefit; speculative land buyers can overpay before final designation or utility verification.

Action: Overlay OZ nominations with JETI thresholds, power, water, labor, rail/highway access, FTZ coverage and local abatements.
Risk check: Texas nominations still require U.S. Treasury certification and JETI requires separate state and school-district approvals.

10 · Border Agriculture · Logistics

Santa Teresa livestock reopening is scheduled for Sep. 24—but screwworm risk is still active

What happened: USDA is preparing to reopen Santa Teresa, New Mexico to Mexican livestock trade on September 24, but a new screwworm case was confirmed in a Grant County, New Mexico horse. Active cases also include a Texas horse and Texas dog.

Why it matters: Livestock-health controls can change cattle flows, inspection times, ranch economics and cross-border agricultural logistics with little notice.

Who may benefit / hurt: Veterinary, biosecurity, tracking and compliant livestock transportation services may see demand; ranchers, feedlots and haulers face treatment and movement risk.

Action: Tighten animal-origin documentation, inspection protocols, wound monitoring and contingency routing ahead of any reopened port movement.
Risk check: USDA had not confirmed whether the newest case would change the September 24 reopening plan. Verify port status immediately before dispatch.
