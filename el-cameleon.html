NOV 25 FIX · MEXICO

Peso falls to 19.7040 per dollar

What happened: Banco de México's official November 25 reference rate is 19.7040 MXN/USD. This was the latest rate displayed on the morning of November 26.

Why it matters: Dollar-funded purchases of peso-priced inputs may cost less, while imported machinery costs more in pesos.

Who may benefit or be hurt: U.S. buyers and Mexican exporters with local costs may benefit; Mexican importers and unhedged dollar borrowers may be squeezed.

Actionable business ideas: Request updated 30-day supplier quotes in both currencies and stress-test budgets at 19, 20 and 21 pesos per dollar.

Risks / verification points: FIX is not a retail or executable quote. Check actual bank spread, contract currency, customs costs and the next official fixing.

Sources: Banco de México · FIX ↗

NOV 24–26 · NEARSHORING

Record FDI masks weaker fresh investment

What happened: Mexico reported $43.09 billion of foreign direct investment for January–September; $34.975 billion came from retained profits and $3.8 billion from new investment. Expansión reported new flows down 42% over the nine-month period; El CEO's November 26 report describes a sharper third-quarter decline.

Why it matters: Headline investment can exaggerate greenfield demand; existing factories are a more promising first sales target.

Who may benefit or be hurt: Maintenance, retrofit and automation vendors may gain; speculative industrial land developers face greater absorption risk.

Actionable business ideas: Build a list of operating foreign-owned plants and pitch uptime, capacity and energy-efficiency upgrades before committing to new land.

Risks / verification points: FDI includes financial flows, not solely capital expenditure. Quarterly and year-to-date comparisons are different; verify individual expansion decisions.

Sources: Expansión · nine-month figures ↗ · El CEO · third-quarter analysis ↗

NOV 25 · TARIFFS

Mexico's deferred Section 301 decision is not an exemption

What happened: USTR issued findings and proposed measures for 14 economies, but did not issue determinations on Mexico or Norway. Written comments and hearing requests are due January 5, 2027.

Why it matters: Regional sourcing may become more competitive if the proposals take effect, but uncertainty remains.

Who may benefit or be hurt: Qualified North American producers may gain inquiries; importers using targeted countries may face higher costs.

Actionable business ideas: Compare suppliers using actual tariff classifications, freight, lead time, quality and origin documentation; submit concrete examples to the docket.

Risks / verification points: Proposals are not effective duties. Verify product annexes, applicable existing duties and whether goods meet USMCA rules; simple transshipment does not establish origin.

Sources: USTR · official proposal ↗

NOV 25 · BRAZIL

Aircraft and sugar buyers review proposed Brazil duties

What happened: USTR proposed changes to its Brazil Section 301 action, including a 10% duty on finished aircraft and changes to out-of-quota sugar duties.

Why it matters: Changes could alter costs for aircraft buyers and food manufacturing supply chains.

Who may benefit or be hurt: Competing domestic producers may benefit; Brazilian suppliers and import-dependent buyers could be hurt.

Actionable business ideas: Model alternate supply contracts, check sugar quota availability and ask brokers to identify product-specific exposure.

Risks / verification points: The measures are proposed; check the final notice, exclusions, quota terms and effective dates before repricing.

Sources: USTR · Brazil proposal ↗

NOV 26 REPORT · SEMICONDUCTORS

TSMC confirms Texas incentive filing, not site selection

What happened: Taipei Times reports that TSMC confirmed its property-tax incentive application Tuesday and said the Texas investment remains under evaluation. Brazos County's November 23 announcement identifies a proposed $22.6 billion fab.

Why it matters: The case shows how tax policy, water, power and workforce can influence North American manufacturing location decisions.

Who may benefit or be hurt: Specialty builders and chip suppliers may gain if selected; speculative developers and taxpayers face downside if projections fail.

Actionable business ideas: Prepare a supplier capability sheet for clean utilities, equipment installation, quality controls and workforce training.

Risks / verification points: A filing is not an award, investment decision or hiring commitment. Verify the final site, incentive terms and procurement channels.

Sources: Taipei Times · company confirmation ↗ · Brazos County · official proposal ↗

NOV 25 · GULF COAST MANUFACTURING

New Orleans small manufacturer plans precision-production upgrade

What happened: Louisiana Economic Development announced an investment of more than $860,000 at Alta Max, with seven projected new jobs and 37 retained positions. Facility work includes HVAC, backup power and fire protection.

Why it matters: Small existing-plant upgrades can create manageable supplier opportunities without a multibillion-dollar project.

Who may benefit or be hurt: Regional commercial trades and precision-manufacturing vendors may benefit; firms without required qualifications may be excluded.

Actionable business ideas: Contact the project owner through public vendor channels about commercial HVAC, electrical and facility compliance services.

Risks / verification points: Investment and hiring are projected, not awarded work; completion is targeted for summer 2029. Check bid scope and eligibility.

Sources: Louisiana Economic Development · announcement ↗

NOV 25 RELEASE · ENERGY

U.S. oil inventories rise while crude prices jump

What happened: EIA's November 25 release covers the week through November 20; commercial crude stocks rose around 1.1 million barrels. Reuters reported Wednesday WTI settled at $83.68 per barrel.

Why it matters: Oil price and inventory signals can diverge as supply risk, refinery demand and trade conditions change.

Who may benefit or be hurt: Upstream suppliers may benefit from stronger prices; trucking and fuel-intensive producers may face cost pressure.

Actionable business ideas: Review fuel escalators, inventory turnover and price scenarios instead of fixing a long-term budget to one trading session.

Risks / verification points: Weekly stocks are estimates and national data. WTI is a dated settlement, not today's physical fuel quote.

Sources: EIA · weekly petroleum report ↗ · Reuters · November 25 settlement ↗

NOV 9 DATA · MEXICO TRADE

Next Mexican merchandise trade release is due tomorrow

What happened: INEGI's trade page lists November 27 as the next update; the September data show $77.8 billion in exports, of which $72.8 billion were manufactured products.

Why it matters: The next release can help distinguish changes in export value from changes in import demand for intermediate and capital goods.

Who may benefit or be hurt: Export logistics and industrial suppliers may benefit from sustained orders; import-dependent manufacturers remain exposed to currency costs.

Actionable business ideas: Track manufacturing exports, intermediate goods imports and port-level freight before changing staffing or warehouse capacity.

Risks / verification points: September data are historical and nominal; the October report was not yet available at this edition's cutoff.

Sources: INEGI · merchandise trade balance ↗

OCT 2 NOTICE · USMCA

Businesses have a separate USMCA consultation window

What happened: USTR requests comments and hearing participation requests by January 12, 2027, at 11:59 p.m. Eastern for the 2027 joint review.

Why it matters: Companies can document origin compliance, border delays and practical impacts of trade rules independently of tariff proceedings.

Who may benefit or be hurt: Compliant exporters, brokers and manufacturers may benefit from clearer rules; firms with weak documentation risk disputes.

Actionable business ideas: Compile shipment-level examples, anonymized where appropriate, and quantify time and compliance costs for a factual submission.

Risks / verification points: Consultation is not a policy change. Distinguish this docket from the January 5 Section 301 deadline.

Sources: USTR · joint review consultation ↗

NOV 26 CHECK · CUSTOMS

Manufacturers can examine pending FTZ production cases

What happened: Commerce's open-comment list includes aerospace production cases closing December 1, Texas applications closing December 7–23 and a Humacao, Puerto Rico production case closing January 4.

Why it matters: FTZ planning may improve duty timing and export logistics for eligible firms.

Who may benefit or be hurt: Import-intensive manufacturers, brokers and compliance vendors may benefit; smaller firms could find administration uneconomic.

Actionable business ideas: Ask the local grantee and broker for a cost-benefit model comparing direct entry, bonded storage and FTZ use.

Risks / verification points: Applications are pending. FTZs do not automatically eliminate Section 301 duties, origin requirements or other restrictions; Federal Register notices control.

Sources: Commerce · open public comment periods ↗
