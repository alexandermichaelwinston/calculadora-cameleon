Americas Opportunity Brief

Every item separates the fact pattern from the business inference. Verify tariff classifications, incentive eligibility, legal treatment and project timing before committing capital.

1 · Mexico · Steel Trade

Mexico opens anti-dumping probe into Japanese steel sheet

What happened: Mexico opened an anti-dumping investigation into steel sheet originating in Japan after Grupo Acerero alleged unfair pricing and injury to domestic producers. Interested parties have 23 business days to enter the process.

Why it matters: A future duty could change the landed-cost gap between Japanese material and North American steel, with downstream effects for fabricators, appliance, auto and construction supply chains.

Who may benefit / hurt: Mexican mills and regional suppliers could gain pricing power; importers dependent on Japanese sheet could face higher cost or sourcing disruption.

Action: identify SKUs using Japanese-origin sheet, model alternate Mexico/U.S./Canada supply and preserve documentation for the investigation window.
Risk check: no definitive duty has been imposed yet; product scope and final margins must be confirmed in Mexico’s official proceeding.
Reuters · Sep. 2
2 · USMCA · September Round

Washington becomes the next USMCA decision point

What happened: U.S. and Mexican negotiators directed their teams to hold a fourth bilateral round in Washington in September after July talks covered economic security, labor, agriculture, payments, steel/aluminum and autos.

Why it matters: Rules of origin and treatment of third-country content can directly change factory economics and supplier qualification across North America.

Who may benefit / hurt: Companies with auditable North American content may gain; import-heavy manufacturers using non-party components face more policy risk.

Action: run a BOM-origin audit and create a 2027 quote scenario with stricter regional-content assumptions.
Risk check: September positions are negotiations, not final law; do not price a proposed rule as certain.
USTR · Jul. 23
3 · Mexico · Foreign Investment

Record FDI masks a weak greenfield pipeline

What happened: Reuters reported Mexico received almost $35 billion in foreign investment in the first half of 2026, but only 7.8% was new investment; greenfield investment fell sharply in 2025 amid USMCA and legal uncertainty.

Why it matters: Existing plants are reinvesting, but fewer brand-new projects means nearshoring is becoming more selective and site underwriting matters more.

Who may benefit / hurt: Existing operators, proven industrial markets and flexible landlords may benefit; speculative parks and suppliers relying on nonstop greenfield growth may be exposed.

Action: favor build-to-suit, phased capex and lease flexibility over a single bullish nearshoring assumption.
Risk check: FDI data are preliminary and total investment includes reinvested earnings, not just new factories.
Reuters · Sep. 1
4 · Currency · Dollar / Peso

Stronger dollar and higher yields change cross-border margins again

What happened: The U.S. dollar reached a two-week high on Sep. 2 as oil, geopolitical risk and expectations for tighter U.S. monetary policy lifted Treasury yields.

Why it matters: FX moves alter the real labor, freight and equipment cost of a Mexico strategy even when local wages do not move.

Who may benefit / hurt: U.S. buyers can regain some purchasing power when the dollar strengthens; Mexican exporters with dollar revenue and peso costs may see margin shifts in the opposite direction when the peso weakens.

Action: use FX adjustment bands, 30-day quote expirations and currency clauses on cross-border contracts.
Risk check: currency moves are fast and reversible; do not extrapolate a one-day move into a long-term rate.
Reuters · Sep. 2
5 · North America · Autos

Canada auto-tariff threat could reroute North American sourcing

What happened: Reuters reported a proposed 50% U.S. tariff on Canadian car imports could hit Toyota and Honda heavily because they dominate Canadian vehicle production.

Why it matters: Auto plants operate through tightly linked U.S., Mexico and Canada supplier networks; a tariff on one country can shift sourcing, assembly and component demand elsewhere.

Who may benefit / hurt: U.S. and Mexican suppliers with available capacity could win rerouted work; Canadian plants and suppliers risk reduced volumes if the proposal takes effect.

Action: identify components you can dual-source in Texas/Mexico and track OEM capacity before January planning cycles lock.
Risk check: the tariff is proposed, not effective policy; companies should model scenarios rather than assume closure or relocation.
Reuters · Aug. 31
6 · U.S. Manufacturing · Labor

August payroll data show manufacturing still shedding jobs

What happened: ADP reported only 38,000 U.S. private-sector jobs added in August, while manufacturing lost 17,000 positions; construction added 12,000.

Why it matters: Manufacturers are balancing automation, high borrowing costs and uncertain demand even while construction and AI-linked investment remain active.

Who may benefit / hurt: Automation, maintenance and productivity vendors may gain; labor-dependent plants with weak order books remain vulnerable.

Action: review revenue per labor hour, overtime, scrap and machine utilization before committing to new headcount.
Risk check: ADP is not the official BLS payroll report and sector figures can be revised.
Reuters · Sep. 2
7 · Texas · Foreign-Trade Zones

Several Texas FTZ decisions are live this month

What happened: The FTZ Board currently lists a proposed Hithium Tech USA subzone in Forney with comments due Sep. 8, CGT automotive-upholstery production in New Braunfels due Sep. 14, and Abbott diagnostic-device production in Irving due Sep. 15.

Why it matters: FTZ treatment can improve duty timing and, for qualifying products, change the duty economics of imported inputs used in U.S. production.

Who may benefit / hurt: Import-intensive manufacturers and their logistics providers may benefit; operations without sufficient duty exposure may not justify compliance overhead.

Action: run an FTZ landed-cost analysis before signing the warehouse or factory lease, not after operations begin.
Risk check: FTZ savings are product- and customs-specific; Section 232/301 goods may require privileged foreign status.
U.S. FTZ Board · current docket
8 · Mexico · Incentive Site Selection

Plan México still matters — but incentives cannot replace site fundamentals

What happened: Mexico’s Plan México and development-pole strategy seek to channel public/private investment into strategic manufacturing, infrastructure and higher domestic content through 2030.

Why it matters: Companies considering Mexico can potentially combine federal/state incentives with industrial clusters, but power, water, security and logistics remain the real gating items.

Who may benefit / hurt: Projects matching strategic sectors and designated regions can gain support; sites chosen only for tax incentives can underperform operationally.

Action: score candidate locations on labor, electricity, water, highway/rail, customs access, security and incentives in that order.
Risk check: every incentive is project-specific; verify eligibility and legal terms with the issuing authority before underwriting value.
Reuters · Plan México investment framework
9 · Americas · Mining Corridors

Argentina and Chile revive cross-border mining framework

What happened: Argentina and Chile moved to revive a treaty framework that can let mining projects share infrastructure and reach Pacific export routes; Chile said the framework could unlock more than $20 billion of investment.

Why it matters: Copper, lithium and related infrastructure are strategic inputs for electrification, data centers and advanced manufacturing throughout the Americas.

Who may benefit / hurt: Engineering, logistics, mining services and port operators could gain; projects remain exposed to commodity prices, permitting and political execution.

Action: track service contracts around roads, power, water, camps, transport and port handling rather than only the mine equity itself.
Risk check: framework-level investment estimates are not committed capex and individual projects require separate approvals.
Reuters · Aug. 27
10 · Energy · Logistics

Oil near the mid-$90s raises the cost floor for freight and construction

What happened: Renewed Middle East conflict pushed Brent around the mid-$90s on Sep. 2 and lifted global bond yields as markets priced more inflation risk.

Why it matters: Fuel, asphalt, chemicals, plastics, trucking and borrowing costs can all hit manufacturer and contractor margins at once.

Who may benefit / hurt: Energy producers and service firms may gain; transport-intensive manufacturers, builders and importers face higher variable costs.

Action: refresh fuel surcharges, escalation clauses, bid validity periods and inventory assumptions for Q4 work.
Risk check: geopolitical energy premiums can reverse quickly; hedge operational exposure rather than trying to forecast oil perfectly.
Reuters · Sep. 2 markets
