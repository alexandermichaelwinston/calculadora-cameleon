Facts are separated from operator inference. Verify tariff classifications, incentive eligibility, labor rules, FX exposure, project timing and local approvals before committing capital.

1 · Energy · Shipping

Oil nears six-week highs as U.S.–Iran attacks disrupt Middle East shipping

What happened: Reuters reported on September 7 that Brent was around $97.17 and WTI around $92.27 as attacks on vessels and sharply slower tanker traffic through the Strait of Hormuz raised supply risk. OPEC+ kept October output policy unchanged.

Why it matters: Higher crude quickly raises diesel, freight, asphalt, plastics, chemicals and working-capital requirements across North American manufacturing and construction.

Who may benefit / hurt: Upstream producers, oilfield services and some refiners may benefit; trucking, builders, food distributors and fuel-intensive manufacturers face margin pressure.

Action: Reprice fuel surcharges, freight allowances, escalation clauses and bid-validity windows for Q4 work.
Risk check: Geopolitical premiums can reverse quickly if shipping conditions improve; do not underwrite a permanent $90+ oil deck from one week of conflict.
Reuters · Sep. 7
2 · USMCA · Autos + Steel

Mexico keeps pressing Washington for auto and steel tariff relief

What happened: Mexico Economy Minister Marcelo Ebrard met U.S. Commerce Secretary Howard Lutnick on September 2 to push for progress on automotive and steel tariffs as the USMCA review process continues.

Why it matters: Autos, steel and aluminum cross the U.S.–Mexico border repeatedly. Changes in tariff treatment or regional-content rules can alter plant economics, sourcing and supplier awards.

Who may benefit / hurt: Suppliers with well-documented North American content may gain; assemblers relying heavily on non-regional components remain exposed.

Action: Price 2027 business under three cases: current tariffs, partial relief and tighter North American content enforcement.
Risk check: Negotiating positions are not final law. Verify the applicable HTS code, USMCA rule of origin and any product-specific exclusion before quoting savings.
Reuters · Sep. 2
3 · Rates · Dollar

Strong U.S. jobs raise September rate-hike risk even as the dollar softens today

What happened: Reuters reported September 7 that markets put roughly a 57–58% probability on a September Fed hike after the strong August jobs report. UBS now expects 25-basis-point hikes in September and December; the dollar index was softer ahead of inflation data.

Why it matters: A higher U.S. rate path raises financing costs for factories, warehouses and inventory while a stronger dollar can reduce the dollar cost of imported equipment but pressure U.S. exporters.

Who may benefit / hurt: Cash-rich buyers and importers may gain relative bargaining power; leveraged developers, exporters and working-capital-heavy manufacturers are more exposed.

Action: Run capex and inventory plans at both a higher-rate/stronger-dollar case and a pause/weaker-dollar case.
Risk check: This week’s PPI and CPI can reset expectations quickly; rate forecasts are not policy commitments.
Reuters · Sep. 7Reuters FX · Sep. 7
4 · Peso · FX

The peso remains exceptionally strong; no new FIX was published for September 7

What happened: Banco de México shows no new FIX for September 7; the latest official FIX remains 16.8748 pesos per dollar from September 4, with the payment rate shown at 16.9560 for September 7.

Why it matters: A strong peso raises the dollar cost of Mexican wages, rent and local inputs while lowering the peso cost of imported machinery and components.

Who may benefit / hurt: Mexican plants importing capital equipment may benefit; exporters earning dollars while paying peso costs can see margin compression.

Action: Use FX bands, shorter quote-validity periods and automatic currency-adjustment clauses in cross-border contracts.
Risk check: Do not treat retail app quotes as an official FIX; currency can move quickly on Fed, oil or USMCA headlines.
Banco de México · FX
5 · Nearshoring · FDI

Mexico’s record FDI still masks a weak pipeline of genuinely new factories

What happened: Reuters reported September 1 that Mexico drew roughly $35 billion of first-half FDI, but only 7.8% represented new investment. Greenfield investment had weakened sharply as companies waited for more USMCA certainty.

Why it matters: Nearshoring is not disappearing, but the winning locations are increasingly existing industrial clusters with proven labor, power and logistics rather than speculative greenfield land.

Who may benefit / hurt: Serviced industrial parks and established suppliers may outperform; land plays depending on a rapid wave of new factory announcements face more risk.

Action: Stage Mexico capex, negotiate expansion options and compare total delivered cost against Texas and other nearshore locations.
Risk check: Headline FDI includes reinvested earnings and should not be treated as new-factory spending.
Reuters · Sep. 1
6 · Mexico · Steel

Mexico’s anti-dumping probe into Japanese steel sheet creates a sourcing watch list

What happened: Mexico opened an anti-dumping investigation into steel sheet from Japan after Grupo Acerero alleged unfair pricing and injury to domestic industry.

Why it matters: A final duty could change landed cost for fabricated metal, construction, appliance and automotive supply chains using covered sheet.

Who may benefit / hurt: Mexican and North American mills could gain pricing power; importers dependent on Japanese-origin sheet could face higher costs.

Action: Map every SKU that uses potentially covered Japanese sheet and qualify U.S., Mexican or Canadian alternatives before annual contracts renew.
Risk check: No final duty exists yet. Product scope, producer-specific margins and the final resolution must be verified before changing suppliers.
Reuters · Sep. 2
7 · Foreign-Trade Zones

Texas FTZ deadlines hit September 8, 14 and 15

What happened: The U.S. Foreign-Trade Zones Board lists Hithium Tech USA in Forney with comments due September 8, CGT in New Braunfels due September 14, and Abbott Laboratories in Irving plus a Texas Ascentec production request due September 15.

Why it matters: FTZ structures can improve cash flow through duty deferral and re-export treatment and can materially change landed-cost economics for import-intensive factories.

Who may benefit / hurt: Manufacturers, customs brokers, bonded logistics and warehouse-management providers may benefit; low-import-volume firms may not justify compliance overhead.

Action: Run an FTZ model SKU by SKU before signing a long factory or distribution lease.
Risk check: HTS classification, Section 232/301 treatment and privileged-foreign status can eliminate theoretical savings.
U.S. FTZ Board
8 · Opportunity Zones 2.0

Texas submitted 605 Opportunity Zone 2.0 tracts to Treasury on September 4

What happened: Texas says it submitted 605 census tracts in 105 counties to the U.S. Treasury on September 4 for the next Opportunity Zone cycle, which begins January 1, 2027.

Why it matters: The new map can influence industrial, housing and mixed-use site economics for a decade, especially where OZ benefits stack with strong utilities, logistics and state/local incentives.

Who may benefit / hurt: Patient developers and operating companies in fundamentally strong tracts may benefit; speculative buyers relying on tax status alone may overpay.

Action: Overlay the Texas nomination map with power, water, workforce, highways, rail, FTZ coverage, JETI eligibility and local abatements before making land offers.
Risk check: Nominated tracts are not final until Treasury certification; tax treatment is fact-specific and requires professional advice.
Texas Opportunity Zones
9 · Border Logistics · Pharr

Pharr bridge expansion is set to begin phased commercial-truck operations in mid-September

What happened: Pharr International Bridge officials said expanded commercial lanes are expected to begin phased operations in mid-September, ahead of a November 12 formal opening.

Why it matters: Additional throughput can shift warehouse, produce cold-chain, customs-brokerage, truck-service and industrial-land economics across the Rio Grande Valley.

Who may benefit / hurt: Cold storage, transload, trucking, brokers and nearby industrial sites may benefit; congested competing routes could lose some volume at the margin.

Action: Map sites within practical drayage range of Pharr and identify bottlenecks in cold storage, truck parking, inspection support and produce handling.
Risk check: Real throughput depends on phased lane openings, inspection staffing and traffic patterns—not just physical bridge capacity.
Texas Border Business · Sep. 3
10 · Border Logistics · Laredo

Colombia Solidarity Bridge starts a 90-day extended-hours program September 14

What happened: CBP’s Port of Laredo and Nuevo León’s border-development corporation plan to open one hour earlier—7 a.m. instead of 8 a.m.—for tractors and empty trailers on weekdays through a 90-day pilot starting September 14.

Why it matters: Even a one-hour operating extension can improve empty-equipment repositioning, morning congestion and fleet utilization across the Laredo freight ecosystem.

Who may benefit / hurt: Drayage carriers, yards, trailer pools and brokers may benefit; value depends on actual carrier adoption and northbound processing performance.

Action: Rework dispatch windows and empty-trailer repositioning schedules around the new 7 a.m. opening and measure dwell-time savings during the pilot.
Risk check: The pilot applies to tractors and empty trailers, not all commercial traffic, and may not become permanent.
CBP release via PublicNow · Sep. 2
