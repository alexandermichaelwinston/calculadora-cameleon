Nov 9 · Jack / Johnson counties

Three Texas gas plants included in NRG's $4 billion agreement

What happened: NRG agreed to sell six plants totaling 4.9 GW to LS Power; the Texas portion includes Jack County, Johnson County and R.W. Miller ERCOT facilities.

Why it matters: Ownership change may reshape equipment procurement, outage planning and operating vendor relationships across North and Central Texas.

Who may benefit or be hurt: Texas turbine-maintenance, HVAC, electrical and controls businesses may see vendor opportunities; incumbents may face contract reassessment.

Business move: Identify facility operators and procurement portals; present turnaround, inspection and parts credentials before ownership transition.
Risks / verification: No verified new-build contract or completed sale; closing targets Q2 2027 subject to regulators and other conditions.

Nov 9 · Amarillo jobs

Tyson's planned second shift could rehire and reskill Amarillo workers

What happened: Tyson said its planned Allied Beef collaboration supports a phased second-shift restart at the Amarillo plant after earlier single-shift restructuring.

Why it matters: The restart would strengthen meat-processing throughput and regional trucking and cold-chain demand if cattle supplies support it.

Who may benefit or be hurt: Workers, cattle producers, maintenance crews and refrigerated transport firms may benefit; smaller competitors may struggle for livestock or labor.

Business move: Track direct Tyson postings and approach livestock haulers, refrigeration vendors and workforce boards for confirmed contract demand.
Risks / verification: Earlier restructuring affected jobs; Nov 9 plans do not establish headcount restored, guaranteed start dates or signed contracts.

Nov 9 · Texas grid / data centers

Scarce gas turbines make power-plant rehabilitation a Texas contracting niche

What happened: Reuters describes long new-turbine delivery queues and renewed interest in used industrial turbines and repurposed aircraft engines as data-center power demand increases.

Why it matters: ERCOT-region builders may need parallel plans for gas supply, electrical interconnection and emissions controls.

Who may benefit or be hurt: Texas generator repair, civil, electrical and permitting firms may gain; data-center developers and communities could incur costs.

Business move: Create a vendor package for inspection, switchgear, controls, sound mitigation and permitting on retrofit projects.
Risks / verification: Used equipment is not automatically reliable, permitted or quick to connect; verify manufacturer service histories and local emissions limits.

Current · Laredo trade corridor

DSV's Laredo hub connects trucking, customs and FTZ warehousing

What happened: The operator identifies 853,000 square feet of warehousing and over 105,000 square feet of bonded/FTZ space at its Laredo cross-border complex.

Why it matters: Importers and exporters can consolidate fulfillment, documentation and transportation near the Texas–Mexico border.

Who may benefit or be hurt: Local drayage, packaging, security and warehouse automation firms may gain; smaller facilities could face competitive pressure.

Business move: Compare broker and bonded-space rates, book facility tours and request throughput KPIs before pitching volume.
Risks / verification: Claimed capacity is not the same as immediately available space; validate cross-border wait times and customs service coverage.

Wing's U.S. drone-manufacturing investment includes a Fort Worth facility

Nov 5 correction · Garwood FTZ

Garwood gas-generation FTZ request carries a corrected 2% finished-product rate

What happened: A Nov 5 correction to FTZ docket B-141-2026 specifies a 2% finished-product duty-rate description for a proposed Garwood natural-gas engine-generation facility; comments close Dec 7.

Why it matters: Imported engines, alternators and steel parts can require careful landed-cost and tariff modeling for power construction.

Who may benefit or be hurt: Customs advisers, engine packagers and compliant contractors may benefit; projects using unsupported duty assumptions may be harmed.

Business move: Review the exact corrected Federal Register notice and prepare a customs model by component and origin.

Samsung Electro-Mechanics' Wilmer subzone remains open for December 14 comment

Dec 1 deadline · Texas tax refunds

Enterprise Zone nominations require local-government action before December 1

What happened: Texas lists December 1, 2026 for the next Enterprise Zone round. A Texas community, not a private company acting alone, must nominate the project for potential qualified state sales/use-tax refunds.

Why it matters: Eligible job-creating industrial projects may recover some qualified sales and use tax after approvals and expenditures.

Who may benefit or be hurt: Manufacturers with eligible capital/job commitments and local partners may benefit; speculative developments lacking job documentation may not qualify.

Business move: Contact economic-development staff now; assemble wage, employment, veteran/resident and capital-budget documentation for screening.
Risks / verification: Nominations are competitive, refunds require tax paid and approved jobs, and application fees and deadlines apply; not an up-front property-tax abatement.

Nov 2026 · Texas site selection

Texas's 608 nominated Opportunity Zone tracts await federal certification

What happened: The Governor reports 608 nominated tracts across 106 counties for the OZ 2.0 period scheduled to begin January 1, 2027; Treasury certification is expected by November 28.

Why it matters: Industrial real estate, workforce housing and rural development site selection can shift if particular tracts are certified.

Who may benefit or be hurt: Eligible fund investors and qualifying communities could benefit; sellers marketing unapproved parcels as designated zones risk misleading buyers.

Business move: Overlay the nomination map with floodplain, utilities, zoning, industrial freight access and tax counsel's fund-qualification checklist.
Risks / verification: Nomination does not guarantee certification or investor eligibility; distinguish capital-gains treatment from county property-tax relief.
