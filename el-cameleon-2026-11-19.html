NOV 18 • TRADE TALKS

U.S.–Mexico tariff negotiations progress, but no agreement is signed

What happened: President Sheinbaum reported advanced talks after meeting President Trump on November 18; relief on steel, aluminum and automotive duties remains a Mexican priority.

Why it matters: Actual duty rates determine landed costs and factory sourcing decisions throughout North America.

Who may benefit or be hurt: Regional suppliers and brokers could gain from clarity; current tariff payers remain exposed and protected producers might face new competition.

Actionable business idea: Model product-specific duty scenarios and add tariff-change clauses to long-term purchase orders.
Risks / verification: Negotiations are not enacted tariffs; confirm HTS treatment and CBP instructions before revising bids.

NOV 18 • AUTO STRATEGY

Nissan uses Mexico to learn how to compete with Chinese automakers

What happened: Nissan's Americas chairman told Reuters on November 18 it is looking to Mexico's competitive auto market for lessons in automation, simpler engineering and supply-chain savings.

Why it matters: North American auto suppliers face growing pressure to increase productivity while maintaining origin compliance.

Who may benefit or be hurt: Automation providers and efficient components makers may gain; high-cost plants and vendors could lose business.

Actionable business idea: Pitch measurable process improvements to tier-one and tier-two automotive suppliers.
Risks / verification: The interview is strategic guidance, not a confirmed new factory or procurement award.
Reuters · Nov 18 ↗
NOV 18 FILING • SOLAR SUPPLY

Tesla proposes a $3.1 billion Texas polysilicon refinery

What happened: Tesla applied for a Texas JETI tax limitation for a potential polysilicon refinery near Robstown; its filing anticipates construction during 2027–2030 and as many as 515 operating jobs by 2032.

Why it matters: A U.S. source of high-purity silicon could alter Asian import dependence and generate industrial construction work.

Who may benefit or be hurt: Process equipment and Gulf Coast contractors could benefit; overseas suppliers may face competition.

Actionable business idea: Prepare industrial piping, chemical-process, electrical and safety qualification packages.
Risks / verification: Tesla says its Texas site choice depends on incentives. Application figures are proposals, not approved funding or awarded contracts.

NOV 18 FIX • PESO

Official dollar/peso FIX rises to 18.6723

What happened: Banco de México reports a November 18 FIX of 18.6723 pesos per dollar, versus 18.4677 the prior business day.

Why it matters: Currency changes affect peso-denominated payroll and dollar-denominated cross-border purchases.

Who may benefit or be hurt: Dollar earners with peso costs may gain; peso earners owing dollars could face higher bills.

Actionable business idea: Recalculate payables at 18.47, 18.67 and 19.00 and evaluate contract currency matching.
Risks / verification: FIX is a reference rate, not a live spot price or guaranteed retail exchange quote.

NOV 19 • THERMAL MANUFACTURING

Johnson Controls targets $179 million in Schertz manufacturing and 552 jobs

What happened: November 19 reporting describes York International/Johnson Controls' planned Schertz facility and investment of at least $179 million, with 552 targeted jobs by 2029 and a possible performance-based $3.75 million local grant.

Why it matters: Advanced HVAC and thermal-management capacity serves factories and data centers.

Who may benefit or be hurt: Industrial technicians, equipment installers and local firms could benefit; competing employers face labor pressure.

Actionable business idea: Qualify for plant controls, electrical, fabrication and workforce-training opportunities.
Risks / verification: Jobs and grant payments depend on future performance; announced plans are not purchase orders.

NOV 17 VOTE • TAX ABATEMENT

County declines SpaceX tax-abatement hearing as air permit advances

What happened: Cameron County voted 3–2 on November 17 not to issue notice for a hearing on SpaceX's proposed county property-tax abatement; the Texas environmental agency issued an air permit for the Starbase computing and power site.

Why it matters: Major industrial investments depend on multiple independent permitting and tax decisions.

Who may benefit or be hurt: Construction suppliers may gain if developed; county taxpayers and neighbors bear fiscal or environmental exposure.

Actionable business idea: Follow separate city and county meetings and ask for verified utility, hiring and tax-impact plans.
Risks / verification: The procedural county vote neither cancels the project nor resolves the separate City of Starbase request.

NOV 13 DATA • BORDER FREIGHT

U.S.–Mexico September freight hits $96.3 billion, with $75.4 billion by truck

What happened: BTS released September data on November 13: $96.3 billion of U.S.–Mexico trade value, up 34.2% year over year, with $75.4 billion transported by truck.

Why it matters: Truck crossings underpin automotive, electronics and industrial inventory networks.

Who may benefit or be hurt: Carriers, customs brokers and secure yards may gain; shippers face delay and security costs.

Actionable business idea: Compare Laredo, Ysleta and Eagle Pass corridors using inspection and total landed-cost data.
Risks / verification: Nominal September value does not establish November truck counts, tonnage, freight rates or warehouse demand.

AUG 24 DATA • NEARSHORING

Mexico's record first-half FDI is mostly reinvested earnings

What happened: Mexico reported first-half 2026 foreign direct investment of $34.968 billion: 88.5% reinvested earnings and 7.8% new investment.

Why it matters: The headline number is not equivalent to new greenfield factory capital.

Who may benefit or be hurt: Existing-facility service vendors may gain while speculative new industrial parks may face absorption risk.

Actionable business idea: Prospect plant retrofits and equipment upgrades before underwriting new warehouse speculation.
Risks / verification: The data cover January–June and do not prove November construction or lease commitments.

NOV 17 • U.S. INDUSTRY

Factory output climbs even as overall industrial production falls

What happened: The Federal Reserve reports October manufacturing output rose 0.3%, but mining fell 0.8% and utilities 4.3%, making total industrial production decline 0.4%.

Why it matters: Industrial investment prospects differ sharply by subsector.

Who may benefit or be hurt: Factory automation providers may gain; mining-related vendors may face slower orders.

Actionable business idea: Prioritize manufacturing customers with confirmed capex and maintenance budgets.
Risks / verification: Preliminary national statistics can be revised and do not guarantee local contract demand.
Federal Reserve G.17 · Nov 17 ↗
DECEMBER • FOREIGN-TRADE ZONES

Three Texas FTZ dockets have approaching comment deadlines

What happened: The Commerce FTZ Board lists pending Garwood gas-equipment, Wilmer Samsung electronics and Harlingen Penn Aluminum cases with December 7, 14 and 23 comment deadlines, respectively.

Why it matters: Zone scope affects import processing and product-level duty economics.

Who may benefit or be hurt: Qualified manufacturers and customs advisers may gain; other producers may be affected by competition.

Actionable business idea: Review the Federal Register case notices and estimate customs exposure by actual HTS code before commenting.
Risks / verification: An open docket is not an approval or blanket duty exemption.
