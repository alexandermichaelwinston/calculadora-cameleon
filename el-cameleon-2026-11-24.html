NOV 23–24 · NORTH AMERICAN CHIP SUPPLY

TSMC weighs $22.6B semiconductor investment in Texas

What happened: Brazos County announced a JETI application on November 23 for a potential advanced logic-chip fab near College Station. Reuters reports Arizona remains a competing site.

Why it matters: A domestic advanced-chip campus could deepen North American electronics sourcing and demand for specialized materials.

Who may benefit or be hurt: Chemical, gas and equipment vendors may gain; competing locations and smaller employers may face recruitment pressure.

Actionable business idea: Qualify for semiconductor supply chains and compare Texas and Arizona logistics costs before reserving capacity.
Risks / verification: The application is not an award or final investment decision. Confirm approvals, funding, water and power availability and procurement releases.

NOV 23 · MEXICO / ASIA TRADE

Mexico and China establish a trade working group

What happened: Mexico’s economy ministry announced a bilateral working group after ministerial talks in Beijing. Mexican officials want faster agricultural sanitary approvals and increased exports to China.

Why it matters: Diversified markets could reduce dependence on a single destination, but imported inputs remain subject to North American origin rules.

Who may benefit or be hurt: Food exporters, refrigerated carriers and certification providers may benefit; competing exporters and import-sensitive manufacturers face uncertainty.

Actionable business idea: Build a product-by-product export readiness checklist covering sanitary access, cold chain, buyer credit and traceability.
Risks / verification: A working group is not a free-trade agreement or a market-access approval. Do not assume Asian inputs acquire Mexican origin through transit.
Reuters · Nov 23 ↗
NOV 23 · MEXICO MACROECONOMY

Mexico’s third-quarter GDP rises 0.9% from Q2

What happened: Reports citing INEGI’s November 23 release show real output up 0.9% quarter on quarter, with secondary activities up 0.4% and services up 1.2%.

Why it matters: Expansion continues, but slower quarterly growth than Q2 favors staged investments rather than indiscriminate factory expansion.

Who may benefit or be hurt: Maintenance and service providers may benefit; highly leveraged developers risk slower lease-up.

Actionable business idea: Pilot production or distribution capacity with anchor-customer commitments and flexible leases.
Risks / verification: GDP is backward-looking and revisable. Compare seasonally adjusted series consistently; national averages do not predict a specific industrial park.
El Nominal / INEGI · Nov 23 ↗El País · Nov 23 ↗
NOV 23 · CURRENCY

Official peso FIX reaches 19.1648 per dollar

What happened: Banco de México published a November 23 FIX of 19.1648 MXN per USD, compared with 19.0228 in the prior Friday edition.

Why it matters: Peso-denominated supplier bills may cost fewer dollars, while dollar debts and imported inputs can become more expensive in pesos.

Who may benefit or be hurt: Dollar-funded buyers may benefit; Mexican importers and borrowers with unhedged USD liabilities may be hurt.

Actionable business idea: Request paired peso and dollar quotes and stress-test exchange rates of 18, 19 and 20 MXN/USD; agree on quote validity and payment dates.
Risks / verification: FIX is a wholesale-derived reference, not an executable retail quote. Actual spreads, forward rates, tariffs and supplier repricing may offset savings.

NOV 23 · U.S.–MEXICO TARIFF WATCH

Mexico confirms plans for another presidential trade meeting

What happened: Mexico’s foreign minister confirmed plans for a meeting in Florida in coming weeks; November 19 reporting described discussions over auto, steel and aluminum duties.

Why it matters: The outcome could affect regional sourcing and border margins, but existing obligations remain until rules change.

Who may benefit or be hurt: Automotive suppliers and metal importers may gain from relief; protected domestic producers could face competition if tariffs fall.

Actionable business idea: Maintain a landed-cost model by tariff code, country of origin and entry date; prepare alternate sourcing and change-in-duty clauses.
Risks / verification: No agreement or tariff change was established by the announcement. Check CBP instructions and controlling legal notices before pricing shipments.
El País · Nov 23 ↗Reuters · Nov 19 ↗
NOV 24 · NEARSHORING / WAREHOUSING

Dallas industrial rent growth highlights regional distribution demand

What happened: A November 24 CommercialCafe report using Yardi data puts Dallas in-place industrial rent growth at 7.7% year over year in October and identifies Mexico nearshoring as one demand driver.

Why it matters: Cross-border manufacturing and e-commerce can create demand for consolidation, returns and distribution capacity away from the border.

Who may benefit or be hurt: Established landlords and logistics operators may gain; new tenants and speculative developers face cost or vacancy risks.

Actionable business idea: Compare cross-dock, shared warehouse and dedicated lease economics across Laredo, Dallas and Houston.
Risks / verification: Market research is not a lease quote or proof that an individual building is occupied. Verify submarket vacancy, concessions and tenant commitments.
CommercialCafe / Yardi · Nov 24 ↗
NOV 24 · COLOMBIA / INVESTMENT

Colombia investment roadshow draws healthcare and infrastructure interest

What happened: Industrial Info reports a $2.6B Patria commitment across healthcare, infrastructure and energy following Milagro Week, plus interest in a potential $200M plasma-center investment.

Why it matters: The announcements point to possible demand for clinical infrastructure, industrial equipment and project finance in South America.

Who may benefit or be hurt: Qualified suppliers and healthcare infrastructure firms may gain; speculative entrants face financing and regulatory risk.

Actionable business idea: Seek local licensed partners and track named projects, financing milestones and formal tenders rather than aggregate headlines.
Risks / verification: Commitments and expressions of interest are not disbursed capital or awarded contracts. Verify local health licensing and individual project status.

NOV 13 RELEASE · SEPTEMBER FREIGHT

U.S.–Mexico freight reaches $96.3B in September

What happened: BTS reported U.S.–Mexico goods freight of $96.3B in September 2026, up 34.2% versus September 2025; trucks carried $75.4B. Canada–Mexico combined U.S. transborder freight totaled $161.1B.

Why it matters: Truck-heavy growth supports border warehouses, customs compliance, yard capacity and intermodal improvements, but also increases bottleneck exposure.

Who may benefit or be hurt: Carrier networks, brokers and industrial landlords may gain; shippers face delay, security, insurance and equipment costs.

Actionable business idea: Evaluate Laredo and El Paso cross-dock utilization and develop a rate-and-dwell-time dashboard before leasing warehouse space.
Risks / verification: These are September trade-value statistics released November 13, not November daily load counts; figures can be revised and price effects influence dollar values.
U.S. BTS · Nov 13 release ↗
OCT 2 NOTICE · USMCA 2027 REVIEW

USTR sets January 12 deadline for USMCA business comments

What happened: USTR opened public input for the 2027 USMCA joint review and set January 12, 2027, at 11:59 p.m. Eastern for comments, hearing requests and testimony summaries.

Why it matters: The consultation gives manufacturers and importers a documented channel to identify rules-of-origin, customs and competitiveness problems.

Who may benefit or be hurt: Firms with substantiated operational examples may influence debate; unprepared suppliers risk failing to document practical trade barriers.

Actionable business idea: Gather tariff-classification examples, cost impacts and cross-border delays; prepare factual, non-confidential comments under the notice's filing rules.
Risks / verification: A submission is not a guarantee of policy change, and public filings may disclose sensitive information. Confirm current hearing instructions on USTR.
USTR · Oct 2 official notice ↗
NOV 24 CHECK · U.S. FOREIGN-TRADE ZONES

Three Texas FTZ dockets listed with December comment deadlines

What happened: Commerce’s current list includes Garwood production (B-141-2026, Dec 7), Wilmer Samsung subzone (S-479-2026, Dec 14) and Harlingen Penn Aluminum production (B-143-2026, Dec 23).

Why it matters: The filings illustrate how industrial operators can pursue location-specific customs arrangements.

Who may benefit or be hurt: Customs brokers, compliance software providers and qualified manufacturers may benefit; competitors may face cost differences.

Actionable business idea: Identify the zone grantee and model inventory, admissions and duty treatment with a customs specialist.
Risks / verification: A pending application is not authorization or universal tariff relief. Individual Federal Register notices control deadlines and scope.
